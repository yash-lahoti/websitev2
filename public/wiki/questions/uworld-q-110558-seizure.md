**Prompt:** A 60-year-old man with a history of renal cell carcinoma presents with his first-ever seizure, which began focally in the left arm and then generalized. He has persistent mild left upper extremity weakness post-ictally. His glucose and sodium levels are normal. What is the best next step?

**Answer:** **Obtain neuroimaging (preferably MRI)**.
The combination of a first-time unprovoked seizure, focal onset, persistent focal neurologic deficit, and a history of malignancy are "red flags" that strongly indicate a structural cause (e.g., brain metastases), requiring detailed imaging.
