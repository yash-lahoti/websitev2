# Hypertension Lifestyle Modification

## Overview
All patients with hypertension should implement lifestyle interventions to reduce blood pressure, regardless of whether they require pharmacologic therapy.

## Comparison of Nonpharmacologic Interventions
The following table summarizes the approximate reduction in systolic blood pressure (SBP) for major lifestyle modifications:

| Intervention | Recommended Plan | Approx. $\Delta$ SBP (mm Hg) |
|---|---|---|
| **DASH Diet** | High in fruits, vegetables, legumes, low-fat dairy; low in saturated/total fats | **~11** |
| **Aerobic Exercise** | 30 min/day, 5+ days/week | ~7 |
| **Sodium Restriction** | $< 1.5-2.3$ g/day | 5-8 |
| **Weight Loss** | Reduction of BMI to $< 25$ kg/m$^2$ | ~6 per 10-kg loss |
| **Alcohol Limitation** | $\le 2$ drinks/day (men), $\le 1$ drink/day (women) | ~5 |

## The DASH Diet
- **What it is:** Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension.
- **Components:** Rich in fruits, vegetables, legumes, low-fat dairy; low in saturated and total fats.
- **Efficacy:** One of the most effective nonpharmacologic interventions, providing a significant reduction in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure.

## Other Considerations
- **Smoking Cessation:** While critical for reducing overall cardiovascular risk, it has a small and inconsistent effect on blood pressure itself.
- **Resistance Training:** Aerobic exercise is consistently effective; however, isometric resistance training has not shown a similar effect on blood pressure.
