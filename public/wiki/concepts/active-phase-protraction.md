# Active Phase Protraction

## Summary
Active phase protraction occurs during the first stage of labor when the cervix dilates at a rate slower than expected during the active phase.

## Definitions
- **First Stage of Labor:** From onset of contractions to 10 cm cervical dilation.
- **Active Phase:** Specifically 6 cm to 10 cm cervical dilation.
- **Protraction Criteria:** Dilation rate is **< 1 cm every 2 hours** during the active phase.

## Common Causes
1. **Cephalopelvic Disproportion (CPD):** The fetal head is too large to fit through the maternal pelvis.
    - Risk factors: Late-term pregnancy ($\ge$ 41 weeks), fetal malposition (e.g., occiput posterior), fetal anomalies.
2. **Inadequate Contractions:** Uterine activity is insufficient to drive dilation.
    - Adequate contractions are typically defined as **$\ge$ 200 Montevideo units** over 10 minutes.
3. **Maternal Factors:**
    - Obesity / excessive weight gain.
    - Nulliparity (generally slower progress than multiparous patients).
    - Advanced maternal age.

## Clinical Pearls
- **Neuraxial Anesthesia (Epidural):** Can lengthen the **second stage** of labor (10 cm to delivery) but typically does **not** cause protraction of the **first stage**.
- **Occiput Posterior Position:** A common malposition that increases the risk of protraction and CPD.

## Management
- Ensure contractions are adequate (e.g., via IUPC and oxytocin if needed).
- If contractions are adequate but progress is still protracted, evaluate for CPD or fetal malposition.
