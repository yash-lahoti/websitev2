# Bright Red Blood Per Rectum (BRBPR) Evaluation

## Overview
Minimal BRBPR is characterized by small amounts of bright red blood on the toilet paper after wiping or a few drops of blood in the toilet bowl. It is most commonly caused by benign anal canal disorders, but can be a sign of more serious pathology.

## Risk Stratification
The choice of diagnostic study depends on the risk of serious disease (e.g., proctitis, polyps, colorectal cancer).

### Low Risk
**Criteria:**
- Younger age (< 45 years).
- Minimal bleeding (no blood mixed with stool).
- No systemic symptoms (fever, weight loss).
- No change in bowel habits or stool caliber.
- Normal digital rectal exam.

**Initial Step:** **Anoscopy**.
- Non-invasive, requires no preparation, can be done in-office.
- Used to visualize the anal canal for hemorrhoids or fissures.

### High Risk
**Criteria:**
- Age $\ge 45$ years (standard CRC screening age).
- Large amount of blood or blood mixed with stool.
- Systemic symptoms (fever, weight loss).
S- Change in bowel habits or stool caliber.

**Initial Step:** **Colonoscopy**.
- Gold standard for direct visualization of the entire colon.

## Management of Benign BRBPR
If anoscopy confirms benign causes (e.g., hemorrhoids):
- **Conservative therapy:** Increased fiber intake, topical analgesics.
- **Follow-up:** Periodic interval monitoring.
