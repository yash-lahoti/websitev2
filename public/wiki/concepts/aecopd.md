# Acute Exacerbation of COPD (AECOPD)

## Summary
Worsening of dyspnea and/or sputum production over <14 days. Often triggered by infection (70%).

## Presentation / Exam Clues
- **Cardinal Symptoms:** $\uparrow$ dyspnea, $\uparrow$ sputum volume, $\uparrow$ sputum purulence.
- **Physical Exam:** Bilateral low-pitched wheezing, prolonged expiration, tachypnea.
- **Imaging:** Hyperinflated lungs, prominent bronchial markings (without infiltrates).

## Workup / Management
- **Immediate:** Supplemental $O_2$ (target $SpO_2$ 88-92%), nebulized bronchodilators.
- **Inflammation:** Systemic glucocorticoids (reduce bronchial wall edema).
- **Infection:** Empiric antibiotics (Fluoroquinolones or 3rd gen Cephalosporins) indicated if:
    1. Patient requires hospitalization (Severe AECOPD).
    2. Grossly purulent sputum (dark green, malodorous).
- **Ventilation:** NIPPV or invasive mechanical ventilation if respiratory failure occurs.

## Common Traps
- **Macrolides:** Poor activity against typical bacteria; not recommended as monotherapy for severe AECOPD.
- **Cultures:** Respiratory cultures have poor diagnostic value; treatment is empiric.
- **Mucolytics:** (e.g., N-acetylcysteine) May help thin secretions but do not improve outcomes.
- **ICS:** Inhaled corticosteroids provide no additional advantage during acute episodes if systemic steroids are used.
