# Leriche Syndrome (Aortoiliac Occlusion)

## Summary
Leriche syndrome is a form of peripheral artery disease (PAD) characterized by atherosclerotic occlusion at the bifurcation of the aorta into the common iliac arteries.

## Clinical Triad
1. **Bilateral Claudication:** Pain in the hips, thighs, and gluteal muscles during exertion.
2. **Diminished Femoral Pulses:** Absent or reduced pulses due to proximal occlusion.
3. **Erectile Dysfunction (ED):** Resulting from restricted blood flow through the internal iliac and internal pudendal arteries.

## Key Diagnostics & Management
- **Initial Test:** Ankle-brachial index (ABI) to evaluate for PAD.
- **Significance of ED:** In this context, ED is often a "warning sign" of significant systemic atherosclerosis and may precede symptomatic coronary artery disease (CAD) by 3-5 years.
- **Management:** Risk factor modification (smoking cessation, BP control) and potential vascular intervention.

## Common Traps
- Attributing ED solely to psychosocial causes or hormonal deficiency (low testosterone) when claudication is present $\rightarrow$ always screen for vascular etiology.
- Confusing with neurologic causes of ED (e.g., spinal injury) $\rightarrow$ neurologic causes do not explain gluteal/thigh claudication.
