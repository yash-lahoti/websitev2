# Neonatal Polycythemia

## Summary
Neonatal polycythemia is defined as a **hematocrit > 65%** in term neonates. It results in increased blood viscosity, which can impair organ perfusion and lead to various systemic symptoms.

## Etiology
- **Intrauterine Hypoxia:** Leads to increased erythropoietin production. Examples include:
    - Maternal diabetes or hypertension (placental dysfunction).
    - Maternal smoking.
    - Intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR).
- **Excessive Transfusion:**
    - Delayed umbilical cord clamping.
    - Twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome.
- **Genetic/Metabolic:**
    - Trisomy 21.
    - Thyroid dysfunction (hyper- or hypothyroidism).

## Clinical Presentation
- **Skin:** Plethoric or ruddy appearance.
- **Neurologic:** Lethargy, irritability, jitteriness.
- **Respiratory:** Tachypnea, respiratory distress, cyanosis, or apnea (due to hyperviscosity).
- **Metabolic:** Hypoglycemia and hypocalcemia (increased RBC mass increases cellular uptake of glucose/calcium).
- **Other:** Poor feeding, abdominal distension.

## Management
- **Hydration:** First-line treatment using oral feeds or glucose-containing parenteral fluids.
- **Partial Exchange Transfusion:** Indicated for severe symptomatic polycythemia.

## Common Traps
- Confusing with Transient Tachypnea of the Newborn (TTN) $\rightarrow$ TTN shows increased pulmonary markings on CXR; polycythemia has a **normal CXR**.
- Misinterpreting hypoglycemia $\rightarrow$ In this context, it is often due to the high red cell mass consuming glucose, not necessarily a primary endocrine failure.
