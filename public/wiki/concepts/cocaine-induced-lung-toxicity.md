# Cocaine-Induced Lung Toxicity ("Crack Lung")

## Summary
Acute lung injury resulting from the inhalation of cocaine, manifesting as Diffuse Alveolar Hemorrhage (DAH). It is caused by a combination of thermal damage, intense pulmonary vasospasm (ischemia-reperfusion), and direct cellular toxicity.

## Presentation / Exam Clues
- **History:** Recent inhaled cocaine use (crack).
- **Toxidrome:** Sympathomimetic (tachycardia, hypertension, mydriasis, restlessness).
- **Physical Exam:** 
    - **Fingertip burns:** Specific indicator of using hot pipes for inhalation.
    - **Pulmonary:** Inspiratory crackles, rapid-onset dyspnea, hypoxemia.
- **Imaging:** 
    - **CXR/CT:** Diffuse bilateral airspace opacities (ground-glass).
    - **Contrast:** Normal cardiomediastinal silhouette (distinguishes from cardiogenic edema).

## Workup / Management
- **Diagnosis:** Clinical (history + imaging). Hemoptysis may be absent in $\sim 50\%$ of cases.
- **Confirmatory (if needed):** Bronchoscopy with serial lavage showing sequentially bloody vials.
- **Management:**
    - **Supportive:** Oxygen, mechanical ventilation if necessary.
    - **Contraindication:** Anticoagulation is generally contraindicated during active DAH.

## Common Traps
- **vs Cardiogenic Edema:** Differentiated by normal troponin/BNP, normal heart size on CXR, and history of drug use.
- **vs Pulmonary Embolism:** PE presents with clear lungs on CXR and signs of RV strain.
- **vs Pulmonary Fibrosis:** Fibrosis is insidious and shows interstitial thickening, not acute airspace opacification.
