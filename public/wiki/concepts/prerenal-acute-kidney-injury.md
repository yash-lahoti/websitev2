# Prerenal Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

## Summary
A reversible decrease in glomerular filtration rate (GFR) caused by decreased renal perfusion without intrinsic damage to the renal parenchyma.

## Etiologies (Decreased Renal Perfusion)
- **True Volume Depletion:** Hemorrhage, dehydration, diuretic overuse.
- **Decreased Effective Arterial Blood Volume (EABV):** Heart failure, cirrhosis (systemic vasodilation).
- **Intravascular Fluid Displacement:** Sepsis (vascular leak), pancreatitis.
- **Renal Vasoconstriction:** NSAIDs (block prostaglandins), ACE inhibitors (dilate efferent arteriole).

## Presentation / Exam Clues
- **Signs of Hypovolemia:** Dry mucous membranes, tachycardia, hypotension, hypernatremia.
- **Laboratory Findings:**
    - **BUN/Creatinine Ratio:** $> 20:1$ (Urea is reabsorbed with sodium and water).
    - **Urinary Sodium:** $< 20$ mEq/L (RAAS activation $\rightarrow$ Na+ retention).
    - **Fractional Excretion of Sodium (FeNa):** $< 1\%$.
- **Urine Sediment:** **Bland** (acellular, no casts or hematuria).

## Management
- **Immediate Action:** Volume resuscitation with intravenous crystalloids.
- **Goal:** Restore adequate renal perfusion to prevent progression to Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN).

## Common Traps
- **vs ATN:** If hypoperfusion persists, it evolves into ATN. ATN is characterized by **muddy brown granular casts**, higher urine sodium ($> 20$ mEq/L), and a lower BUN/Cr ratio (usually $10-15:1$).
- **vs Glomerulonephritis:** GN presents with hematuria, RBC casts, and proteinuria, unlike the bland sediment of prerenal AKI.
