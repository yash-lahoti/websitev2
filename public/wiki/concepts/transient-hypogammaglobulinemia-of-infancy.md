# Transient Hypogammaglobulinemia of Infancy (THI)

## Summary
A self-limited disorder characterized by a delayed synthesis of IgG, leading to a prolonged physiologic nadir of IgG levels beyond 6 months of age.

## Presentation / Exam Clues
- **Age:** Typically presents between 6 months and early childhood.
- **Symptoms:** 
    - May be asymptomatic.
    - Recurrent respiratory infections (e.g., acute otitis media, bronchiolitis).
    - Atopic features (e.g., eczema, asthma, food allergies).
- **Physical Exam:** Often unremarkable, except for atopic signs (e.g., scaly cheek lesions).

## Workup / Management
- **Laboratory Findings:**
    - **IgG:** Low (isolated or with mild $\downarrow$ IgA/IgM).
    - **B and T cells:** Normal leukocyte counts and lymphocyte subsets.
    - **Vaccine Response:** **Normal** antibody response to vaccines (Crucial for distinguishing from CVID or Agammaglobulinemia).
- **Management:** 
    - Observation is typically sufficient.
    - Prophylactic antibiotics for severe recurrent infections.
    - Spontaneous resolution by early childhood.

## Common Traps
- **vs CVID:** Common Variable Immunodeficiency (CVID) also presents with low IgG, but patients with CVID **fail** to generate a normal antibody response to vaccines.
- **vs X-linked Agammaglobulinemia:** In Bruton's, B-cell numbers are markedly low and all immunoglobulin classes are typically absent/severely low.
