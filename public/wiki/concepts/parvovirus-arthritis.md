# Parvovirus B19 Viral Arthritis

## Overview
Parvovirus B19 infection can present as an acute, symmetric, polyarticular arthritis, particularly in adults. It often mimics Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) but follows a benign, self-limited course.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Joints:** Symmetric arthralgias/arthritis affecting small joints (Hands, wrists, knees, feet).
- **Symmetry:** Typically symmetric.
- **Course:** Self-limited, usually resolving within 1-2 months.
- **Associated Findings:** 
    - **Children:** Fever and "slapped cheek" rash (Erythema Infectiosum).
    - **Adults:** Joint symptoms predominate; rash is often mild or absent.
    - **High-risk patients:** Can trigger aplastic crisis in those with underlying hematologic disease (e.g., sickle cell) due to infection of red cell precursors.

## Differential Diagnosis: Parvovirus vs RA
Both present with symmetric small-joint arthritis, but Parvovirus is distinguished by:

| Feature | Parvovirus B19 | Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) |
|---|---|---|
| **Morning Stiffness** | Brief (e.g., 10-15 mins) | Prolonged ($\ge 30$ mins) |
| **ESR / CRP** | Typically Normal | Typically Elevated |
| **Course** | Self-limited (weeks) | Chronic / Progressive |
| **Exposure** | Children/Daycare | N/A |

## Diagnosis & Management
- **Diagnosis:** 
    - **Acute:** Anti-parvovirus IgM antibodies.
    - **Immunocompromised:** NAAT for B19 DNA.
    - **Past infection:** IgG antibodies.
- **Treatment:** Symptomatic management with NSAIDs.
