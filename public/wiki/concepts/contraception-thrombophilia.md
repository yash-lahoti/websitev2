# Contraception in Thrombophilia

## Overview
Patients with thrombophilias, such as **Antiphospholipid Antibody Syndrome (APS)**, have a baseline increased risk for arterial and venous thrombosis. This risk is significantly amplified by the use of exogenous estrogen.

## Contraindications
**Estrogen-containing hormonal contraceptives** are **absolutely contraindicated** in patients with APS or other thrombophilias. Examples include:
- Combination Oral Contraceptive Pills (COCPs)
- Vaginal rings
- Transdermal patches

## Recommended Alternatives
1. **Nonhormonal Methods:**
   - **Copper-containing IUD:** Highly efficacious; appropriate regardless of uterine position (e.g., retroverted uterus).
   - Permanent sterilization (e.g., tubal ligation).
   - Barrier methods (e.g., condoms), though these have lower typical efficacy.
2. **Progestin-Only Methods:**
   - Progestin-releasing IUDs.
   - Progestin-only pills (minipills).
   - These carry a significantly lower risk of thromboembolism compared to estrogen-containing options.

## Clinical Pearls
- **Uterine Position:** A retroverted or retroflexed uterus does **not** contraindicate IUD insertion nor does it affect the expulsion or failure rates.
- **Hemoglobin:** Copper IUDs may increase menstrual bleeding, making them less ideal for patients with severe baseline anemia, but acceptable for those with normal hemoglobin levels.
