# Pseudocyesis

## Summary
Pseudocyesis is a form of somatization characterized by a persistent, nondelusional belief that one is pregnant, despite medical evidence to the contrary.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Subjective Symptoms:** Patients report classic signs of early pregnancy, including:
    - Amenorrhea
    - Morning sickness (nausea/vomiting)
    - Abdominal distension
    - Breast tenderness and fullness
- **Medical Findings:** 
    - Negative pregnancy tests (urine/blood).
    - Ultrasound revealing an empty uterus (e.g., thin endometrial stripe).
- **Psychological Context:** Often triggered by intense psychological or social pressures, such as extreme difficulty conceiving or a strong desire for motherhood.

## Pathophysiology
- **Mechanism:** Psychological stress can affect the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis, potentially leading to actual amenorrhea or bodily changes (weight gain) that the patient misinterprets.
- **Nature:** It is a nondelusional belief, meaning the patient is not psychotic but is experiencing a somatic manifestation of psychological distress.

## Management
- **Psychiatric Evaluation:** Essential for diagnosis and treatment of the underlying stress or somatization disorder.
- **Supportive Therapy:** Counseling and psychotherapy.

## Differential Diagnosis
| Condition | Key Differentiator |
|---|---|
| **Early Intrauterine Pregnancy** | Positive pregnancy test; gestational sac on ultrasound. |
| **Ectopic Pregnancy** | Positive pregnancy test; empty uterus but often an adnexal mass. |
| **Hydatidiform Mole** | Markedly elevated $\beta$-hCG; "snowstorm" appearance on ultrasound. |
| **Missed Abortion** | History of positive pregnancy test; ultrasound shows nonviable fetus/gestational sac. |
| **Premenstrual Syndrome** | Regular ovulatory cycles; no belief of pregnancy. |
