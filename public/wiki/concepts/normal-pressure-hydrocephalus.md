# Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH)

## Summary
A clinical syndrome characterized by the classic triad of gait instability, cognitive impairment, and urinary incontinence, caused by decreased CSF absorption. It is a potentially reversible cause of dementia.

## The Classic Triad
1. **Gait Instability:** Broad-based, shuffling gait (often the first and most prominent symptom); frequent falls.
2. **Cognitive Impairment:** Memory deficits, impaired executive function, depressed affect (due to frontal lobe compression).
3. **Urinary Incontinence:** Often presents initially as urinary urgency.

## Pathophysiology
- **Mechanism:** Decreased cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) absorption by the arachnoid granulations.
- **Dynamics:** Increased ventricular compliance allows the ventricles to expand without significantly increasing intracranial pressure (hence "normal pressure").
- **Causes:**
    - **Idiopathic:** Associated with chronic periventricular ischemia and increased venous resistance.
    - **Secondary:** Result of neurologic insults such as meningitis or subarachnoid hemorrhage (due to scarring of granulations).

## Diagnosis
- **Imaging (MRI/CT):** Ventriculomegaly (enlarged ventricles) out of proportion to underlying cortical atrophy (sulcal enlargement).
- **Lumbar Puncture (Miller Fisher / Lumbar Tap Test):** Large-volume removal of CSF leads to a marked clinical improvement in gait.
- **CSF Studies:** Normal opening pressure and normal CSF composition.

## Management
- **Definitive Treatment:** Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunting to divert excess CSF.
