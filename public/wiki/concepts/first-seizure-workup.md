# First-Time Seizure Workup in Adults

## Overview
An unprovoked, first-time seizure in an adult requires a systematic evaluation to identify structural, metabolic, or toxic causes.

## Initial Evaluation
1. **Stabilization:** Ensure patient is safe; manage status epilepticus if present.
2. **Metabolic Screen:** Rule out common provokers:
    - **Glucose:** Hypoglycemia (usually < 55 mg/dL).
    - **Electrolytes:** Severe hyponatremia (usually < 120 mEq/L).
    - **Tox Screen:** Alcohol withdrawal, medication toxicity.

## "Red Flags" Indicating Structural Lesions
The presence of any of the following strongly suggests a structural brain lesion (e.g., tumor, stroke, hemorrhage) and necessitates urgent neuroimaging:
- **Focal Onset:** Seizure begins in one area before secondarily generalizing.
- **Focal Neurologic Deficits:** Persistent weakness or sensory loss post-ictally (Todd's paralysis may be transient, but persistent deficits are concerning).
- **Medical History:** History of malignancy (increased risk of brain metastases).
- **Atypical Presentation:** Unusual seizure semiology.

## Neuroimaging Selection
- **CT Scan:** Preferred in the Emergency Department for speed; excellent for detecting acute hemorrhage, large tumors, or major strokes.
- **MRI Brain:** Gold standard for detailed structural evaluation. More sensitive than CT for identifying:
    - Small metastases.
    - Temporal sclerosis.
    - Cortical dysplasia.
    - Vascular malformations.

## Key Clinical Pearl
A seizure can be the **presenting symptom** of brain metastases, even if previous imaging (e.g., during cancer staging) was normal.
