# Massive Hemothorax

## Overview
Massive hemothorax occurs when a large volume of blood accumulates in the pleural space, typically following blunt or penetrating thoracic trauma. Because each hemithorax can hold up to 40% of the total circulating blood volume, this condition frequently presents as **hemorrhagic (hypovolemic) shock**.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Hemodynamic Instability:** Hypotension, tachycardia, flat neck veins (despite fluid resuscitation).
- **Physical Exam:**
    - **Breath Sounds:** Diminished or absent on the affected side.
    - **Percussion:** **Dullness** to percussion (distinguishes it from pneumothorax).
    - **Trachea:** May be deviated **contralaterally** due to mass effect from the blood volume.

## Differential Diagnosis: Hemothorax vs. Tension Pneumothorax

| Feature | Massive Hemothorax | Tension Pneumothorax |
|---|---|---|
| **Percussion** | **Dull** | **Hyperresonant** |
| **Breath Sounds** | Diminished/Absent | Absent |
| **Neck Veins** | Flat (Hypovolemia) | Distended (Obstructive shock) |
| **Tracheal Shift** | Contralateral | Contralateral |
| **Hemodynamics** | Hypotensive (Hemorrhagic) | Hypotensive (Obstructive) |

## Management
1. **Immediate Step:** Tube thoracostomy (chest tube).
2. **Indication for Emergent Thoracotomy:**
   - Immediate bloody drainage of **$>20 \text{ mL/kg}$** (approximately $1,500 \text{ mL}$ in an adult).
   - Continued massive bleeding (e.g., $>200 \text{ mL/hr}$ for 2-4 hours).
