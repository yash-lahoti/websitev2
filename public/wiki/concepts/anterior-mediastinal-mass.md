# Anterior Mediastinal Mass

## Summary
Anterior mediastinal masses often present in young adults with symptoms of mass effect (cough, dyspnea, SVCS) or paraneoplastic syndromes.

## Differential Diagnosis (The "4 Ts")
1. **T**hymoma: Typically middle-aged; associated with Myasthenia Gravis.
2. **T**errible Lymphoma: Often high-grade; may have systemic B-symptoms (fever, weight loss).
3. **T**hyrd (Thyroid) Neoplasm: Retrosternal goiter or thyroid cancer.
4. **T**eratoma / Germ Cell Tumors (GCTs): Common in young men.

## Germ Cell Tumor (GCT) Differentiation
GCTs are differentiated primarily by serum markers:

| Tumor Type | $\beta$-hCG | AFP | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seminoma** | $\uparrow$ (some) | Normal | Most common GCT; AFP is essentially always normal |
| **Nonseminomatous (NSGCT)** | $\uparrow$ | $\uparrow$ | Includes Yolk Sac, Choriocarcinoma, Mixed GCTs; usually both or either elevated |
| **Benign Teratoma** | Normal | Normal | May show fat, bone, or fluid on CT |

## Workup and Management
- **Imaging:** CT chest is the gold standard for characterization.
- **Surgical/Diagnostic:** Biopsy for confirmation.
- **Additional Screening:** Testicular ultrasound is mandatory to check for primary testicular GCTs, as management differs based on the primary site.
