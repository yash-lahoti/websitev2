# Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS)

## Summary
Ankylosing spondylitis is a chronic inflammatory arthropathy primarily affecting the axial skeleton, characterized by inflammatory back pain and potential systemic complications.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Inflammatory Back Pain:** Insidious onset < 40 years, symptoms > 3 months, relieved by exercise (not rest), and often presents as nocturnal pain.
- **Physical Findings:** 
    - Impaired spinal mobility and reduced chest expansion.
    - **Enthesitis:** Tenderness at tendon insertion sites (e.g., bilateral heel pain/Achilles tendon).
    - Other: Dactylitis ("sausage digits"), Uveitis.
- **Labs/Imaging:** HLA-B27 association; elevated ESR/CRP; sacroiliitis on X-ray or MRI.

## Cardiovascular Complication: Aortic Regurgitation
Chronic inflammation can extend to the aortic root and valve cusps, leading to:
- **Pathophysiology:** Retraction of valve cusps $\rightarrow$ impaired closure $\rightarrow$ Aortic Regurgitation (AR).
- **Clinical Sign:** LV volume overload $\rightarrow$ eccentric hypertrophy $\rightarrow$ **lateral displacement of the PMI**.
- **Prevalence:** Occurs in up to 10% of AS patients.
- **Other:** Rare conduction blocks if inflammation involves the interventricular septum.

## Common Traps
- Confusing reduced chest expansion with the cause of dyspnea $\rightarrow$ In AS, reduced expansion contributes, but severe exertional dyspnea and displaced PMI suggest heart failure from AR.
- Thinking other valves are involved $\rightarrow$ AR is the primary valvular complication; mitral and tricuspid valves are generally not affected.
