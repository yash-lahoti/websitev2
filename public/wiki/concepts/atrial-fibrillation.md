# Atrial Fibrillation (AF)

## Summary
AF is characterized by an irregularly irregular ventricular rhythm and an absence of organized P waves on ECG. In new-onset AF, it is critical to identify potentially reversible triggers.

## Risk Factors & Pathophysiology
- **Atrial Remodeling:** Increased by advanced age, hypertension, mitral valve disease, heart failure, CAD, obesity, and obstructive sleep apnea.
- **Automaticity Triggers:** Hyperthyroidism, excessive alcohol use, acute illness (sepsis, PE, MI), and cardiac surgery.

## Workup for New-Onset AF
- **Thyroid Function:** Serum TSH and free T4 levels should be checked in all patients to rule out hyperthyroidism.
- **Structural Evaluation:** Transthoracic echocardiogram (TTE) to evaluate for valvular disease or LV dysfunction.
- **Trigger Search:** Evaluate for acute triggers if the patient is young or has no known chronic risk factors.

## Management Note
Hyperthyroidism-induced AF often resolves with treatment of the underlying thyroid condition.
