# Prevalence vs Incidence

## Definitions
- **Incidence:** The number of **new cases** of disease that develop in a specific population over a specified time period.
- **Prevalence:** The proportion of a population that has the disease at a **particular point in time** (existing cases).

## The Relationship
In a stable population (negligible growth and minimal migration), the relationship can be approximated as:
$$\text{Prevalence} \approx \text{Incidence} \times \text{Average Duration of Disease}$$

## Clinical Implications
- **Stable Incidence, Increasing Prevalence:** This occurs when the **duration of the disease increases**. This is typically due to:
    - Improved disease management.
    - Better quality of care.
    - Increased life expectancy for patients with the disease.
- **Stable Incidence, Decreasing Prevalence:** This occurs when the duration of disease decreases (e.g., higher mortality rate or more effective cures).
- **Increasing Both:** Occurs when the underlying risk or diagnostic accuracy increases.
