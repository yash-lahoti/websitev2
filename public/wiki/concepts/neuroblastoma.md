# Neuroblastoma

## Summary
Neuroblastoma is the most common extracranial solid tumor of childhood (median age < 2 years). It arises from **neural crest cells**, which are precursors to the sympathetic ganglia and adrenal medulla.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Common Sites:** Adrenal medulla (most common) and the sympathetic chain.
- **Regional Effects:**
    - **Abdominal Mass:** Often presents as a palpable abdominal mass.
    - **Orbital Metastases:** Periorbital ecchymoses ("raccoon eyes").
    - **Spinal Cord Compression:** Epidural invasion resulting in "dumbbell" tumors.
    - **Paraganglionic involvement:** If the tumor arises in the cervical paravertebral sympathetic chain, it can lead to **ipsilateral Horner syndrome** (ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis).
- **Systemic Symptoms:** Irritability, fevers, pallor.
- **Opsoclonus-Myoclonus Syndrome:** "Dancing eyes, dancing feet" (paraneoplastic syndrome).

## Diagnostics
- **Catecholamines:** Elevated urine and serum vanillylmandelic acid (VMA) and homovanillic acid (HVA) in the majority of patients.
- **Histology:** Small, round, blue cells.
- **Genetics:** NMYC gene amplification (indicates poor prognosis).
- **Definitive Diagnosis:** Tissue biopsy.

## Common Traps
- Confusing with Pheochromocytoma $\rightarrow$ Pheos usually occur in adults and present with hypertension and paroxysmal headaches.
- Mistaking Horner syndrome for Bell palsy $\rightarrow$ Bell palsy involves the facial nerve (VII) and causes facial droop, but **does not** affect pupillary response or sweating (anhidrosis).
