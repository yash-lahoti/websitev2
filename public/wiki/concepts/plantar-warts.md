# Plantar Warts (Verruca Plantaris)

## Summary
Plantar warts are benign epithelial tumors caused by infection with the Human Papillomavirus (HPV). They typically present as hyperkeratotic papules on the soles of the feet.

## Pathophysiology
- **Etiology:** Human Papillomavirus (HPV).
- **Transmission:** Direct skin contact with an infected individual; the virus enters through small cuts or abrasions.
- **Incubation:** Lesions may develop weeks to months after the initial exposure.
- **Risk Factors:** Children, young adults, and immunocompromised individuals (e.g., AIDS, organ transplant recipients).

## Clinical Presentation
- **Morphology:** Hyperkeratotic, cauliflower-like (verrucous) papules.
- **Location:** Bottom of the foot (plantar surface).
- **Key Diagnostic Feature:** Shaving the hyperkeratotic surface reveals **pinpoint bleeding** from thrombosed capillaries.
- **Symptoms:** May be asymptomatic or cause pain during walking.

## Management
- **Spontaneous Resolution:** Many warts resolve on their own.
- **Active Treatment:** Indicated for symptomatic or persistent lesions.
    - **Cryotherapy:** Liquid nitrogen.
    - **Chemical Cautery:** Salicylic acid.

## Differential Diagnosis
| Condition | Key Differentiator |
|---|---|
| **Callus** | Larger, non-discrete borders; forms at bony prominences due to friction; no pinpoint bleeding. |
| **Lichen Planus** | Flat-topped, polygonal, violaceous (purple) papules/plaques; often pruritic. |
| **Molluscum Contagiosum** | Poxvirus; dome-shaped, pink/skin-colored papules with **central umbilication**. |
| **Seborrheic Keratosis** | "Stuck-on" brown papules; typically occurs in adults > 50; atypical on soles. |
