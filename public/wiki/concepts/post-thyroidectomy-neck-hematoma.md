# Post-thyroidectomy Neck Hematoma

## Summary
A post-thyroidectomy neck hematoma is a critical surgical complication where blood accumulates in the deep neck spaces, potentially leading to rapid and lethal airway obstruction.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Early Signs:** "Tight" feeling in the neck, subtle swelling under the incision.
- **Advanced Signs:** 
    - Stridor.
    - Dysphagia.
    - Voice changes.
    - "Tripod positioning" (indicating respiratory distress).
- **Cautions:** Patients may maintain normal peripheral oxygen saturation ($\text{SpO}_2$) until very shortly before complete airway collapse (< 1 min).

## Management
- **Immediate Action:** **Decompress the airway**. This involves reopening the incision and evacuating the hematoma (can be done at the bedside).
- **Definitive Care:** 
    - Move to the operating room for exploration and control of the bleeding source.
    - Evaluate for laryngeal edema; consider protective endotracheal intubation.
- **Contraindications:**
    - **Do NOT** apply compressive dressings (this can increase pressure on the airway and worsen obstruction).
    - **Do NOT** delay treatment with imaging (e.g., CT scan).

## Comparison: Neck Hematoma vs. Other Post-op Issues
| Feature | Neck Hematoma | Laryngeal Edema |
|---|---|---|
| **Onset** | Rapid / Early | Often more gradual |
| **Physical Exam** | Ballotable swelling, "Tight" neck | No mass, but stridor/dyspnea |
| **Primary Tx** | Evacuation of blood | Intubation / Steroids |
