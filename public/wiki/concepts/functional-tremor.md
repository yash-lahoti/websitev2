# Functional Tremor

## Overview
A **functional tremor** (formerly known as a psychogenic tremor) is an involuntary movement disorder where the tremors are not consistent with a known organic neurological cause. These tremors often occur in the context of psychiatric comorbidities, but can also appear in patients without known psychopathology.

## Key Clinical Features
Functional tremors are characterized by **inconsistency** and **variability**:
- **Abrupt Onset:** Often starts suddenly with a static course.
- **Distractibility:** Tremors typically decrease or cease entirely when the patient is distracted (e.g., during a physical exam or while performing a different task).
- **Inconsistency:** 
    - May be present at rest and during movement.
    - Can have a shifting frequency (e.g., frequency changes when the patient taps an uninvolved limb).
    - The tremor may "chase" the restraint (moving to a different location when the affected limb is held).
    - Patients may be able to perform complex tasks (e.g., writing words) but fail others (e.g., drawing spirals).
    - Fingers are often spared.

## Differential Diagnosis

| Tremor Type | Characteristics | Distinguishing Feature from Functional |
|---|---|---|
| **Essential Tremor** | Action tremor (worst with outstretched arms), usually suppresses at rest. | Consistent frequency; not distractible. |
| **Cerebellar Tremor** | Low frequency, high amplitude; increases as target is approached (intention). | Associated with ataxia/dysmetria; consistent. |
| **Physiologic Tremor** | Low amplitude, fast (10-12 Hz); augmented by anxiety/caffeine. | Consistent, low amplitude; worsens with movement. |
| **Orthostatic Tremor** | Affects legs and trunk only when standing. | Specific to posture; not distractible. |

## Management
- **Compassionate Education:** Validate the patient's experience (the symptoms are involuntary and not "in their head") while explaining the functional nature of the disorder.
- **Therapy:** Physical and occupational therapy, including intensive inpatient rehabilitation.
