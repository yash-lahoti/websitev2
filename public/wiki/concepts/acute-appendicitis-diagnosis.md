# Acute Appendicitis Diagnosis

## Overview
Acute appendicitis is typically characterized by migratory pain (periumbilical $\rightarrow$ RLQ), nausea, vomiting, fever, and leukocytosis. While clinical suspicion was historically sufficient for surgery, imaging is now standard to reduce negative appendectomy rates (NAR).

## Recommended Imaging
The choice of imaging depends on the patient population:

| Population | Preferred Imaging Study | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Nonpregnant Adults** | **CT scan of the abdomen and pelvis (with contrast)** | Preferred due to high diagnostic accuracy, speed, and reliability. |
| **Children** | **Ultrasound (US) of the abdomen** | First-line to avoid ionizing radiation. |
| **Pregnant Women** | **Ultrasound (US) of the abdomen** | First-line to avoid fetal radiation; MRI is the secondary option if US is nondiagnostic. |

## Management Guidance based on CT
- **Normal Appendix:** Rule out appendicitis and evaluate for other causes of RLQ pain.
- **Nonperforated Appendicitis:** Appendectomy (usually within 12 hours) and antibiotics.
- **Perforated Appendicitis:**
    - **Contained Abscess:** Percutaneous drainage + antibiotics.
    - **Free Perforation:** Emergency appendectomy + antibiotics.
