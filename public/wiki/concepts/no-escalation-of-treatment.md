# No Escalation of Treatment (NEOT)

## Summary
NEOT orders are predetermined limits on medical care that are not to be exceeded regardless of clinical deterioration. They serve as a bridge between aggressive care and the withdrawal of life support.

## Utility in End-of-Life Care
- **Purpose:** Prevents the implementation of futile, high-morbidity interventions (e.g., mechanical ventilation, hemodialysis) while allowing the family time to process an imminent loss.
- **Psychological Benefit:** Lessens the family's feelings of guilt associated with "withdrawing" care or "hastening" death, as the focus is on "not starting" futile treatments.
- **Clinical Application:** Most useful when families are reluctant to transition immediately to comfort measures but recognize that further escalation is futile.

## Contrast with Other Philosophies
| Philosophy | Key Characteristic | Outcome/Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **NEOT** | Predetermined upper limits on treatment | Avoids futile interventions; eases psychological transition |
| **Time-limited trial** | Intensive care for a fixed interval (e.g., 2-3 days) | May involve temporary escalation to see if patient stabilizes |
| **Withdrawal of life support** | Complete transition to comfort measures only | Focuses on comfort; removes existing life-sustaining therapy |
