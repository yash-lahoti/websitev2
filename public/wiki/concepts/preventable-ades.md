# Preventable Adverse Drug Events (ADEs)

## Summary
Preventable ADEs are a significant cause of outpatient visits and hospitalizations. They often stem from a combination of provider errors (e.g., vague instructions) and patient factors (e.g., low health literacy).

## Common Causes of Preventable ADEs
- **Provider Factors:**
	- **Vague Prescriptions:** Instructions like "as directed" or "as needed" without specifying limits or intervals.
	- **Insufficient Counseling:** Lack of discussion regarding adverse effects, dosing, and drug names.
	- **Illegible Handwriting:** Leads to dosage or medication errors.
- **Patient Factors:**
	- **Low Health Literacy:** Inability to interpret health information for decision-making.
	- **Polypharmacy:** Increased risk of interactions and errors.
	- **Communication Barriers:** Language or cognitive gaps.
- **Medication Factors:**
	- **Look-alike / Sound-alike Drugs:** (e.g., prednisone vs. prednisolone).

## Corrective Strategies
- **Plain-Language Teaching:** Brief, clear instructions during prescribing and dispensing. Should include:
	- Generic and brand name.
	- Indication.
	- Duration.
	- Possible adverse effects and interactions.
	- Clear intervals (e.g., "wait 1 hour before second dose") and limits (e.g., "maximum 2 pills per day").
- **Teach-Back Method:** Patient describes the treatment plan in their own words to confirm understanding.
- **Medication Reconciliation:** Increasing checkpoints across multiple providers.
