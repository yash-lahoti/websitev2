# Blunt Abdominal Trauma (BAT) Management

## Overview
The primary goal in the initial management of blunt abdominal trauma is to rapidly identify and control life-threatening hemorrhage.

## Hemodynamic Stability & Decision Algorithm
The next diagnostic or therapeutic step is determined by the patient's hemodynamic stability and physical exam findings.

### 1. Hemodynamically Unstable ($\text{SBP} < 90\text{ mm Hg}$)
Priority is to rule out fatal intrathoracic conditions (Tension Pneumothorax, Cardiac Tamponade) and identify the source of instability.

- **If Frank Peritonitis is present (e.g., rigidity):** $\rightarrow$ **Emergent Laparotomy**.
- **If No Peritonitis $\rightarrow$ Perform FAST Scan:**
    - **Positive FAST (free intraperitoneal fluid):** $\rightarrow$ **Emergent Laparotomy**.
    - **Negative/Inconclusive FAST:**
        - If stability improves with resuscitation $\rightarrow$ **CT scan of abdomen/pelvis**.
        - If remains unstable $\rightarrow$ Consider **Diagnostic Peritoneal Lavage (DPL)**.

### 2. Hemodynamically Stable
- **CT scan of abdomen/pelvis:** The gold standard for identifying the site and severity of intraabdominal injury (e.g., splenic or hepatic lacerations).
- **Angiography/Embolization:** May be indicated for specific lower-grade injuries identified on CT.

## Key Contraindications
- **Vasopressors:** Contraindicated in isolated hemorrhagic shock. Peripheral vascular tone is already maximized via compensatory vasoconstriction. Volume resuscitation (crystalloids, blood products) is the only appropriate treatment.
- **CT Scan in Unstable Patients:** CT is contraindicated in unstable patients due to the risk of rapid deterioration in the scanner where monitoring and intervention are limited.
