# Allergic Conjunctivitis

## Summary
Allergic conjunctivitis is an IgE-mediated hypersensitivity reaction to environmental allergens, resulting in bilateral inflammation of the conjunctiva.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Symmetry:** Bilateral.
- **Key Symptom:** **Ocular pruritus** (intense itching) — a primary differentiator from viral causes.
- **Findings:**
    - Diffuse conjunctival injection (redness).
    - Watery discharge.
    - Eyelid edema.
    - Occasional light crusting upon awakening.
- **Associated Factors:** Often associated with other atopic conditions (e.g., eczema, allergic rhinitis, asthma).
- **Absent Findings:** No pain, no photophobia, and no decrease in visual acuity (these are "red flags" for more serious ocular conditions).

## Management
- **Pharmacotherapy:**
    - **Topical antihistamines** (e.g., olopatadine, ketotifen).
    - **Mast cell stabilizers**.
- **Supportive Care:**
    - **Cool compresses** (induces vasoconstriction; avoid warm compresses as they can worsen histamine-mediated vasodilation).
    - Oral antihistamines if concomitant allergic rhinitis is present.
- **Avoidance:** Identify and avoid triggering allergens.

## Differential Diagnosis
- **Viral Conjunctivitis:** Also watery discharge; but typically lacks intense pruritus and may be unilateral initially.
- **Bacterial Conjunctivitis:** Thick, purulent discharge; often begins unilaterally.
- **Corneal Abrasion:** Severe pain, photophobia, and positive fluorescein stain.
