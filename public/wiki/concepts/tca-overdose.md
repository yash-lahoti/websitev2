# Tricyclic Antidepressant (TCA) Overdose

## Summary
TCA overdose is a potentially lethal event characterized by a triad of anticholinergic effects, CNS toxicity, and cardiovascular instability. The primary cause of mortality is cardiac toxicity leading to arrhythmias and hypotension.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Anticholinergic Effects:** 
    - Hyperthermia, flushed skin, mydriasis (dilated pupils).
    - Dry mouth, urinary retention, decreased bowel sounds (ileus).
- **CNS Toxicity:** 
    - Altered mental status (drowsiness, delirium, coma).
    - Seizures (typically tonic-clonic).
- **Cardiovascular Toxicity:** 
    - Sinus tachycardia and hypotension.
    - **ECG findings:** QRS prolongation (>100 msec) due to sodium channel blockade, prolonged PR and QT intervals.

## Management
1. **Supportive Care:** Secure airway, breathing, and circulation (ABC).
2. **Fluid Resuscitation:** Isotonic saline boluses for hypotension.
3. **Sodium Bicarbonate (IV):**
    - **Indication:** QRS duration >100 msec or ventricular arrhythmias.
    - **Mechanism:** Increases sodium load to overcome sodium channel blockade and alkalinizes serum to reduce TCA activity.
    - **Benefit:** Narrows QRS, reduces risk of ventricular arrhythmia, and may improve hypotension.
4. **Decontamination:** Activated charcoal if within 2 hours of ingestion (provided there is no ileus).
5. **Refractory Hypotension:** May require vasopressors (e.g., norepinephrine).

## Differential Diagnosis
- **Opioid Overdose:** Miosis (pinpoint pupils), respiratory depression, no QRS prolongation.
- **Organophosphate Poisoning:** Cholinergic crisis (bradycardia, miosis, salivation, lacrimation). Treated with atropine and pralidoxime.
- **Hyperkalemia:** Peaked T waves, eventually sine-wave pattern. Treated with calcium gluconate.
