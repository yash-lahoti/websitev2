# Post-ERCP Pancreatitis

## Overview
Post-ERCP pancreatitis (PEP) is the most common complication following endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography. It occurs in up to 10% of patients, with a higher incidence in those with **sphincter of Oddi dysfunction (SOD)**.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Onset:** Typically develops within several hours to days after the procedure.
- **Symptoms:**
    - Severe epigastric pain, often radiating to the back.
    - Nausea and vomiting.
    - Pain exacerbated by eating.
    - Fever may be present due to the inflammatory response.

## Diagnosis
Acute pancreatitis is diagnosed when a patient meets **$\ge 2$ of the following 3 criteria**:
1. **Clinical:** Characteristic severe epigastric pain (radiating to the back).
2. **Laboratory:** Serum **lipase** or **amylase** $\ge 3\times$ the upper limit of normal.
3. **Imaging:** Characteristic findings on CT, MRI, or ultrasound.

### Diagnostic Strategy
- **Initial Workup:** Serum lipase and amylase should be the first step. These enzymes rise quickly after symptom onset.
- **Imaging:** CT scans are highly sensitive for perforation but may remain **normal for up to 48 hours** in cases of pancreatitis. Therefore, they are not the primary tool for the initial diagnosis of PEP unless perforation is suspected.

## Differential Diagnosis (Post-ERCP Pain)
| Diagnosis | Key Distinguishing Features |
|---|---|
| **PEP** | Epigastric pain radiating to back, elevated lipase/amylase. |
| **Perforation** | Rigidity, rebound, guarding, free air on CT. |
| **Cholangitis** | Right upper quadrant pain, jaundice, high fever, hypotension. |
| **Cholecystitis** | RUQ pain, positive Murphy's sign, cystic duct obstruction on HIDA scan. |
