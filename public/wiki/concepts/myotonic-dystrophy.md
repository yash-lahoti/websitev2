# Myotonic Dystrophy (DM)

## Summary
The most common adolescent- and adult-onset muscular dystrophy. It is an autosomal dominant trinucleotide (CTG) repeat expansion disorder of the *DMPK* gene.

## Presentation / Exam Clues
- **Muscular:** 
    - **Facial weakness:** Difficulty making expressions (smiling/frowning), ptosis.
    - **Distal weakness:** Forearm, hand, and ankle musculature.
    - **Myotonia:** Delayed relaxation of muscles after contraction (e.g., grip myotonia, percussion myotonia).
    - **Wasting:** Temporal wasting and emaciated extremities.
- **Non-Muscular Findings:**
    - **Endocrine:** Primary hypogonadism (testicular atrophy in males).
    - **Ocular:** Cataracts.
    - **GI:** Dysphagia, constipation.
    - **Cardiac:** Conduction disturbances, cardiomyopathy.
    - **Other:** Frontal hair loss, insulin resistance, daytime somnolence.

## Workup / Management
- **Genetics:** CTG repeat expansion (Autosomal Dominant).
- **Diagnosis:** Clinical presentation + genetic testing.
- **Management:** Primarily supportive; multidisciplinary care for cardiac and respiratory complications.

## Common Traps
- **vs Duchenne/Becker:** DM involves distal and facial weakness and myotonia, whereas Duchenne/Becker primarily involve proximal weakness and do not feature myotonia.
- **vs Fragile X:** Fragile X features macroorchidism, whereas DM features testicular atrophy.
