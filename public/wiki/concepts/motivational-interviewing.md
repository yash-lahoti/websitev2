# Motivational Interviewing & Stages of Change

## Summary
Motivational interviewing is a nonjudgmental, patient-centered psychotherapeutic technique used to encourage patients to change maladaptive behaviors by focusing on their intrinsic motivation and autonomy.

## Stages of Change Model

| Stage | Patient's State | Physician's Approach (Motivational Interviewing) |
|---|---|---|
| **Precontemplation** | Not ready to change; does not acknowledge negative consequences. | Provide education about the disease process and complications to build motivation. |
| **Contemplation** | Thinking of changing; acknowledges consequences but is ambivalent. | Discuss pros/cons of options; assess **conviction** (importance) and **confidence** (ability). |
| **Preparation** | Ready to change; has decided to change. | Collaborate on a specific action plan; schedule appointments. |
| **Action** | Making change; takes specific, overt actions. | Provide support and tools to maintain the change. |
| **Maintenance** | Changes integrated into life. | Focus on relapse prevention and long-term stability. |
| **Identification** | Behavior is automatic; change is part of sense of self. | Support identity transition. |

## Key Principles
- **Autonomy:** Support the patient's right to choose and take responsibility.
- **Nonjudgmental Stance:** Acknowledge ambivalence and resistance without judgment.
- **Intrinsic Motivation:** Focus on the patient's own reasons for changing rather than external pressure.
- **Avoid Dismissiveness:** Do not use phrases like "Don't worry" or "Forget about it."
- **Avoid Judgment:** Do not imply the patient is "not trying hard enough."
