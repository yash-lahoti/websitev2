# Pleural Effusion Physical Exam

## Overview
Pleural effusion occurs when fluid accumulates in the pleural space. This fluid acts as an insulator between the lung parenchyma and the chest wall, altering the transmission of sound and vibration.

## Physical Examination Findings
The hallmark of pleural effusion is the "insulation" effect.

| Finding | Observation | Pathophysiology |
|---|---|---|
| **Breath Sounds** | $\downarrow$ or absent | Fluid blocks the transmission of breath sounds from the lung to the chest wall. |
| **Tactile Fremitus** | $\downarrow$ | Fluid dampens the vibrations transmitted from the airways. |
| **Percussion** | Dullness | Fluid is denser than air-filled lung, creating a dull sound. |

## Differential Diagnosis of Dullness to Percussion
Dullness to percussion indicates increased density. The key to differentiating is the **tactile fremitus** and **breath sounds**.

| Condition | Breath Sounds | Tactile Fremitus | Percussion | Shift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pleural Effusion** | $\downarrow$ | $\downarrow$ | Dull | Away from effusion |
| **Consolidation** (e.g., Pneumonia) | $\uparrow$ (bronchial) | $\uparrow$ | Dull | None |
| **Atelectasis** | $\downarrow$ | $\downarrow$ | Dull | Toward atelectasis |

## Contrast with Pneumothorax
While both pleural effusion and pneumothorax cause $\downarrow$ breath sounds and $\downarrow$ tactile fremitus, they differ on percussion:
- **Pleural Effusion:** Dullness (Fluid)
- **Pneumothorax:** Hyperresonance (Air)
