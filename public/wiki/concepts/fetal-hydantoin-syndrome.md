# Fetal Hydantoin Syndrome

## Summary
Fetal hydantoin syndrome is a pattern of congenital malformations resulting from in utero exposure to certain antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), most notably **phenytoin**.

## Presentation / Exam Clues
- **Craniofacial:** Microcephaly, wide anterior fontanelle, cleft lip, and/or cleft palate.
- **Extremities:** Hypoplasia of the distal phalanges.
- **Cardiac:** Potential for congenital heart defects (e.g., pulmonary or aortic stenosis).
- **Developmental:** Associated neural tube defects and risk for cognitive impairment/developmental delay.

## Prevention & Management
- **Pre-conception Planning:** Titrate AEDs to the lowest effective dose for seizure control before conception.
- **Supplementation:** High-dose folic acid (**4 mg/day**) to reduce the risk of neural tube defects and other malformations.

## Common Traps
- Confusing with Fetal Alcohol Syndrome: FAS also features microcephaly and midfacial hypoplasia, but **not** cleft lip/palate or distal phalange hypoplasia.
- Confusing with ACE-inhibitor teratogenicity: Lisinopril causes renal failure, oligohydramnios, and Potter sequence, not the craniofacial/extremity findings seen here.
