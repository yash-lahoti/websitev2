# Congenital Rubella Syndrome (CRS)

## Summary
Congenital Rubella Syndrome occurs when the rubella virus is transmitted transplacentally to the fetus, particularly during the first trimester (organogenesis), leading to significant teratogenic effects.

## Clinical Presentation
The classic triad consists of:
1. **Cardiac Defects:** Most commonly **Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA)**, presenting as a harsh, continuous "machine-like" murmur.
2. **Eye Abnormalities:** **Cataracts** (leukocoria) and glaucoma.
3. **Hearing Loss:** Sensorineural hearing loss.

**Other Findings:**
- Fetal growth restriction (IUGR).
- Hepatosplenomegaly.
- "Blueberry muffin" rash (purpuric spots).

## Diagnosis
- **Clinical:** Identification of the classic triad and maternal history of febrile illness/arthralgia in the first trimester.
- **Confirmation:** Positive infant IgM serology.

## Prevention
- Vaccination of women prior to pregnancy.
- Rubella vaccine is a live attenuated vaccine and is contraindicated during pregnancy.

## Differential (TORCH)
- **CMV:** Periventricular calcifications, chorioretinitis, microcephaly.
- **Toxoplasmosis:** Diffuse intracranial calcifications, chorioretinitis, hydrocephalus.
- **Syphilis:** Snuffles, hepatosplenomegaly, osteoarticular destruction.
