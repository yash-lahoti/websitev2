# Testicular Torsion

## Summary
Testicular torsion is a surgical emergency caused by the twisting of the spermatic cord, leading to ischemia and potential necrosis of the testicle. It most commonly occurs in adolescent boys due to rapid testicular growth during puberty, but can occur at any age.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Pain:** Abrupt-onset, severe scrotal pain; often triggered by physical activity or mild trauma.
- **Associated Symptoms:** Nausea and vomiting are common.
- **Physical Exam:**
    - Profoundly tender, enlarged, and erythematous hemiscrotum.
    - **High-riding testicle** (horizontal position).
    - **Absent cremasteric reflex** (classic finding).
- **Late Findings:** Heterogeneous echotexture on ultrasound indicates testicular necrosis (typically >12 hours of ischemia).

## Diagnostics
- **Diagnosis:** Primarily clinical.
- **Imaging:** Doppler ultrasound (shows reduced or absent blood flow and potentially a reactive hydrocele).

## Management
- **Urgent Urology Consultation:** Required for surgical detorsion.
- **Surgical Treatment:** 
    - Detorsion of the affected testicle.
    - **Bilateral orchiopexy** (fixation of both testicles to the tunica vaginalis) to prevent recurrence on the affected side and protect the contralateral side.
- **Manual Detorsion:** Attempted if immediate surgery is unavailable.

## Differential Diagnosis
| Condition | Key Differentiator |
|---|---|
| **Epididymitis** | Gradual onset; fever; positive Prehn sign (elevation of scrotum relieves pain); preserved cremasteric reflex |
| **Torsion of Appendix Testis** | "Blue dot sign"; pain localized to upper pole; preserved cremasteric reflex |
| **Inguinal Hernia** | Groin/abdominal pain; bowel loops on US; normal testicle size |
| **Testicular Tumor** | Painless, slow-growing mass; normal cremasteric reflex |
