# Internuclear Ophthalmoplegia (INO)

## Summary
Internuclear ophthalmoplegia is a disorder of conjugate horizontal gaze caused by a lesion in the **medial longitudinal fasciculus (MLF)**. It is most classically associated with **Multiple Sclerosis (MS)** in young adults.

## Pathophysiology
The MLF is a heavily myelinated tract that coordinates communication between the contralateral abducens nucleus (CN VI) and the ipsilateral oculomotor nucleus (CN III). 
- **Normal function:** To look left, the left CN VI nucleus fires (abducting the left eye) and sends a signal via the **right MLF** to the right CN III nucleus (adducting the right eye).
- **Lesion:** A lesion in the MLF disrupts this signal.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Ipsilateral Adduction Deficit:** The eye on the side of the lesion cannot adduct (look past the midline) during conjugate gaze.
- **Contralateral Abduction Nystagmus:** The opposite eye abducts but exhibits horizontal nystagmus.
- **Preserved Functions:** Convergence and pupillary light reflexes are typically preserved (as they do not rely on the MLF).

## Context: Multiple Sclerosis
- INO is a hallmark finding in MS due to the predilection for demyelinating plaques in the MLF.
- Often presents as part of a larger syndrome of neurologic deficits disseminated in space (e.g., ataxia, vertigo, optic neuritis) and time.
- **Pseudobulbar affect:** Inappropriate laughing/crying can occur due to corticobulbar tract plaques.

## Localization Table
| Finding | Localization |
|---|---|
| Impaired adduction of RIGHT eye | Right MLF lesion |
| Impaired adduction of LEFT eye | Left MLF lesion |
| Impaired abduction of RIGHT eye | Right CN VI (Abducens) nerve/nucleus |
| Impaired abduction of LEFT eye | Left CN VI (Abducens) nerve/nucleus |
