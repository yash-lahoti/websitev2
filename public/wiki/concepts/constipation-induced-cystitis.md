# Constipation-induced Cystitis

## Overview
In young children, **chronic functional constipation** is a significant risk factor for **recurrent cystitis** (lower urinary tract infections).

## Pathophysiology
1. **Rectal Distension:** Chronic withholding of stool leads to a progressively dilated rectum filled with hard feces.
2. **Mechanical Compression:** The distended rectum compresses the bladder and the urethra.
3. **Urinary Stasis:** This compression prevents complete voiding of the bladder, leading to residual urine (urinary stasis).
4. **Bacterial Colonization:** Stagnant urine acts as a breeding ground for bacteria (e.g., *E. coli*) that ascend from the perineum, causing infection.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Cystitis:** Dysuria, urgency, frequency, and suprapubic tenderness.
- **Constipation Signs:** History of stool withholding, hard stools, and anal fissures (due to straining/large stools).
- **Rectal Exam:** Presence of hard stool in the rectal vault.

## Management
To prevent recurrence, both the infection and the underlying cause must be treated:
1. **Treat Cystitis:** Appropriate antibiotics (e.g., trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole).
2. **Treat Constipation:** 
   - Long-term laxative therapy to soften stools.
   - Behavioral modifications to prevent stool withholding.
   - Goal: Allow the rectum to return to normal size and relieve pressure on the urinary tract.

## Differential Note
- **Vesicoureteral Reflux (VUR):** More commonly associated with recurrent **upper** tract infections (pyelonephritis) rather than isolated simple cystitis.
