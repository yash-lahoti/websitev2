# Attrition Bias

## Summary
Attrition bias is a subtype of **selection bias** that occurs when a substantial number of participants are lost to follow-up in a prospective study (e.g., cohort study or RCT), and the reasons for dropping out are related to the outcome or exposure.

## Mechanism
- **Loss to Follow-up:** Participants leave the study before the outcome is measured.
- **Systematic Difference:** If those who drop out differ significantly from those who remain (in terms of risk or outcome), the remaining sample is no longer representative of the original population.
- **Effect:** This can lead to an **overestimation** or **underestimation** of the true association between the exposure and the disease.
    - *Example:* If high-risk individuals in the exposed group drop out due to early onset of the disease, the study may underestimate the relative risk.

## Contrast with Other Biases
- **Selection Bias (General):** Systematic error in the selection of participants such that the sample is not representative of the population.
- **Observer Bias:** Misclassification of data due to the observer's preconceived expectations.
- **Recall Bias:** Subjects with a negative outcome are more likely to remember/report exposures (retrospective studies).
- **Surveillance Bias:** Exposed groups are monitored more closely, leading to higher detection rates.

## Mitigation
- Maximize follow-up rates through rigorous tracking and participant engagement.
- Report all losses to follow-up transparently in study publications.
