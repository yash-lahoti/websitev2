# Bullous Pemphigoid (BP)

## Summary
An autoimmune blistering disorder characterized by the development of tense bullae, most common in patients >60 years old.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Prodrome:** Weeks to months of pruritic, eczematous, or urticarial-like lesions.
- **Characteristic Lesions:** Tense bullae that may rupture to form erosions.
- **Distribution:** Often diffuse or localized to the trunk, extremities, axillae, and inguinal folds.
- **Key Feature:** Pruritus is prominent; mucosal involvement is rare (distinguishes from pemphigus vulgaris).
- **Associated Disorders:** Increased incidence in patients with neurologic disorders (e.g., Parkinson disease), dementia, and psychiatric disorders (e.g., depression, bipolar).

## Workup & Diagnosis
- **Skin Biopsy (Essential):**
	- **Light Microscopy:** Shows subepidermal cleavage.
	- **Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF):** Shows linear IgG and C3 deposition along the basement membrane.
- **Serology:** Autoantibodies against bullous pemphigoid antigens (hemidesmosomes).

## Management
- **First-line:** High-potency topical corticosteroids (e.g., clobetasol). Effective even for extensive disease.
- **Systemic Options:** Oral corticosteroids or oral doxycycline.
