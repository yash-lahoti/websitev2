# Ludwig Angina

## Summary
Ludwig angina is a rapidly progressive, polymicrobial cellulitis of the submandibular and sublingual spaces. It is a surgical emergency due to the high risk of upper airway obstruction.

## Pathophysiology
- **Primary Source:** Most commonly arises from dental infections of the mandibular molars.
- **Spread:** Infection spreads contiguously from the tooth root into the submylohyoid and then the sublingual space.
- **Microbiology:** Polymicrobial; typically a mix of oral aerobes (e.g., viridans streptococci) and anaerobes.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Systemic Symptoms:** Rapid onset of fever, chills, and malaise.
- **Local Compression Symptoms:**
    - Severe mouth and neck pain.
    - Dysphagia and drooling.
    - **Muffled voice** ("hot potato voice").
    - **Tongue displacement:** The tongue is pushed posteriorly and superiorly due to edema of the floor of the mouth.
    - **Submandibular findings:** Tender, indurated, non-fluctuant swelling.
    - **Crepitus:** May be present if gas-producing anaerobes are involved.

## Diagnostics
- **CT Scan (Neck):** The gold standard for confirming the diagnosis and ruling out a fluctuant abscess.

## Management
1. **Airway Management:** Immediate assessment. Early intervention with a mechanical airway may be necessary if respiratory status is impaired.
2. **Medical Therapy:** High-dose intravenous antibiotics (e.g., ampicillin-sulbactam, clindamycin).
3. **Source Control:** Extraction of the inciting infected tooth.
4. **Surgical Drainage:** Rarely required since the process is typically cellulitic (non-suppurative), but indicated if antibiotics fail.

## Differential Diagnosis
| Condition | Key Differentiator |
|---|---|
| **Epiglottitis** | Supraglottic swelling; no floor-of-mouth elevation or submandibular induration. |
| **Tonsillitis** | Inflammation centered on palatine tonsils; no sublingual space involvement. |
| **Parotitis** | Lateral face swelling superficial to the masseter; no tongue displacement. |
