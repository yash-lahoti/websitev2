# Hypoglycemia in Critical Illness

## Summary
Hypoglycemia can occur in non-diabetic patients during sepsis or other critical illnesses. It is primarily driven by excessive glucose consumption in peripheral tissues and impaired hepatic production.

## Mechanisms
- **Increased Tissue Glucose Use:** Promoted by inflammatory cytokines during sepsis.
- **Suppressed Gluconeogenesis:** Sepsis can lead to a reduction in hepatic gluconeogenesis.
- **Renal Insufficiency:** Increases risk due to:
	- Decreased renal gluconeogenesis.
	- Reduced clearance of endogenous insulin.

## Differential Diagnosis (Non-Diabetic Hypoglycemia)
- **Sepsis / Critical Illness**
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** (Often presents with hyponatremia and hypotension non-responsive to fluids alone).
- **Severe Hepatic Failure**
- **Prolonged Starvation / Alcoholism:** Depleted glycogen stores.
- **Drugs:** Quinolones, quinine, beta blockers, ACE inhibitors.
- **Insulinoma / Surreptitious Insulin Use**
