# Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP)

## Summary
Pneumocystis pneumonia is an opportunistic infection caused by *Pneumocystis jirovecii*. It primarily affects individuals with severe deficits in cell-mediated immunity, particularly those with advanced AIDS or those receiving chronic high-dose glucocorticoids.

## Risk Factors
- **AIDS:** Typically occurs when CD4+ T-cell count falls below $200/\text{mm}^3$.
- **Immunosuppressive Therapy:** Chronic glucocorticoid use, especially when combined with other immunosuppressants (e.g., cyclophosphamide).

## Clinical Presentation
- **General:** Fever, chills, fatigue, and a nonproductive (dry) cough.
- **Respiratory:** Progressive dyspnea on exertion, tachypnea, and hypoxia.
- **Acute Presentation:** Patients on immunosuppressive therapy often present with **acute respiratory failure** and respiratory alkalosis.
- **AIDS Presentation:** Often more indolent than those on immunosuppressive meds.
- **Laboratory Findings:** Elevated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) is common.
- **Imaging:** Chest X-ray typically shows **bilateral, diffuse interstitial infiltrates**.

## Diagnostics
- **Confirmation:** Identification of *P. jirovecii* in respiratory samples.
- **Preferred Methods:**
    - Induced sputum.
    - Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid (gold standard for high yield).

## Management
- **Treatment:** A 21-day course of **trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX)**.
- **Adjuncts:** Corticosteroids may be added for severe hypoxia ($\text{PaO}_2 < 70\text{ mmHg}$ or $\text{A-a gradient} > 35\text{ mmHg}$).
- **Prophylaxis:** TMP-SMX is used for primary prophylaxis in patients with severe cell-mediated immunity deficits (e.g., $\text{CD4} < 200$ or chronic high-dose steroids).

## Differential Diagnosis
| Condition | Distinguishing Feature |
|---|---|
| **Diffuse Alveolar Hemorrhage** | Abrupt onset, hemoptysis, common in SLE. |
| **Invasive Aspergillosis** | Fever, chest pain, hemoptysis; nodules or segmental infiltrates on CXR. |
| **SLE-induced Pulmonary Fibrosis** | Progresses over weeks/months; typically lacks fever. |
