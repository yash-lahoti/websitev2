# Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)

## Summary
HCM is an autosomal dominant condition caused by mutations in sarcomere proteins (e.g., myosin-binding protein C, beta-myosin heavy chain). It causes asymmetric hypertrophy of the interventricular septum, leading to **dynamic left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction**.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Symptoms:** Dyspnea, fatigue, chest pain, syncope, and risk of sudden cardiac arrest (SCA).
- **Physical Exam:**
  - Midsystolic/crescendo-decrescendo systolic murmur.
  - Best heard at the left sternal border or apex.
  - Carotid pulse may show a **dual upstroke**.

## Dynamic Murmur Maneuvers
The HCM murmur intensity depends on the size of the LV cavity. **Smaller LV volume $\rightarrow$ increased LVOT obstruction $\rightarrow$ louder murmur.**

| Maneuver | Effect on LV Volume | Effect on HCM Murmur |
|---|---|---|
| **Valsalva (strain phase)** | $\downarrow$ (Decreased preload) | **$\uparrow$ Intensity** |
| **Standing (from supine)** | $\downarrow$ (Decreased preload) | **$\uparrow$ Intensity** |
| **Squatting** | $\uparrow$ (Increased preload & afterload) | $\downarrow$ Intensity |
| **Leg Raise (passive)** | $\uparrow$ (Increased preload) | $\downarrow$ Intensity |
| **Handgrip (sustained)** | $\uparrow$ (Increased afterload) | $\downarrow$ Intensity |

## Contrast with Aortic Stenosis (AS)
- **AS Murmur:** Increases with increased preload (e.g., squatting, leg raise).
- **HCM Murmur:** Decreases with increased preload; increases with decreased preload.

## Common Traps
- Confusing HCM with AS: Remember that Valsalva *increases* HCM murmur but *decreases* AS murmur.
- Forgetting the "dual upstroke" of the carotid pulse in HCM.
