# Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria (PNH)

## Summary
A clonal stem cell disorder characterized by intravascular and extravascular hemolysis, hemoglobinuria, and a hypercoagulable state.

## Presentation / Exam Clues
- **Hemolysis:** Dark urine (hemoglobinuria), anemia, fatigue, dyspnea.
- **Labs:** $\uparrow$ LDH, $\downarrow$ Haptoglobin, $\uparrow$ Indirect bilirubin.
- **Hypercoagulability:** Unusual venous thrombosis, especially intra-abdominal (e.g., hepatic vein, portal vein) or cerebral veins.
- **Cytopenias:** May present with pancytopenia or be associated with aplastic anemia.

## Workup / Management
- **Diagnosis:** **Flow cytometry** assessing for the absence of **CD55** and **CD59** on red blood cell surfaces (due to lack of GPI anchor).
- **Management:**
    - Iron and folate supplementation.
    - **Eculizumab:** Monoclonal antibody that inhibits complement activation.

## Common Traps
- **Acute Intermittent Porphyria:** Also presents with abdominal pain and dark urine, but urine is due to porphyrins (not hemoglobinuria), and thrombosis is rare.
- **G6PD Deficiency:** Hemolytic anemia, but typically triggered by specific agents and not associated with venous thrombosis.
