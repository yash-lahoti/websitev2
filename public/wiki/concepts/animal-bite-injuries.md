# Animal Bite Injuries

## Summary
Wounds caused by animal bites (primarily dogs and cats) that require a combination of surgical wound care, tetanus prophylaxis, and targeted antibiotic therapy based on risk.

## Presentation / Exam Clues
- **Cat Bites:** Typically deep puncture wounds due to long, sharp teeth. High risk of inoculating oral flora deep into soft tissues.
- **Dog Bites:** Often lacerations or crush injuries. Lower risk of deep puncture compared to cats.
- **Key Pathogen:** *Pasteurella multocida* (gram-negative coccobacilli) is common in both, but more critical for cat bite prophylaxis.

## Workup / Management
- **Wound Care:**
    - Copious irrigation with saline.
    - Debridement of necrotic tissue.
    - **Healing:** Typically left open to heal by secondary intention to avoid abscess formation.
    - **Primary Closure:** Avoided unless the wound is on the face (for cosmetics) or involves deep musculoskeletal/vascular structures.
- **Antibiotics (Prophylaxis):**
    - **Indicated for:** All cat bites, infected dog bites, or dog bites in high-risk areas (hands, feet, joints, genitalia) or immunocompromised patients.
    - **Drug of Choice:** **Amoxicillin-clavulanate** (covers *P. multocida* and anaerobes).
- **Tetanus Prophylaxis:** Administer booster if incompletely vaccinated or last dose $\ge 5$ years ago.
- **Rabies Prophylaxis:** If high-risk animal cannot be quarantined/observed.

## Common Traps
- **Cat vs Dog:** Do not assume a "minor" cat bite can be observed; deep punctures always warrant prophylaxis.
- **Clindamycin:** Covers skin flora and anaerobes but **does not** cover *P. multocida*; therefore, not sufficient as monotherapy for bites.
