# Todd Paralysis

## Summary
Todd paralysis is a transient, focal neurologic deficit (typically hemiparesis or hemiplegia) that occurs in the postictal period following a focal seizure (which may secondarily generalize).

## Clinical Features
- **Timing:** Occurs immediately after a seizure.
- **Presentation:** Flaccid weakness or paralysis of one or more extremities, usually on the same side of the body.
- **Course:** Self-limited; symptoms typically resolve gradually within **36 hours**.
- **Pathophysiology:** Likely due to localized neuronal exhaustion/depression in the cerebral cortex.

## Diagnostic Approach
- **Clinical Diagnosis:** History of a preceding seizure followed by focal weakness.
- **Exclusion of Other Causes:** Imaging (CT/MRI) is essential if the preceding seizure was unwitnessed or subtle, to exclude:
    - **Ischemic Stroke:** Focal infarct on imaging.
    - **Intracranial Hemorrhage:** Bleeding on imaging.
- **Imaging in Todd Paralysis:** Typically **normal**.

## Comparison: Todd Paralysis vs. Stroke vs. Hemiplegic Migraine
| Feature | Todd Paralysis | Ischemic Stroke | Hemiplegic Migraine |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Preceding Event** | Seizure (focal or generalized) | Risk factors (HTN, AFib) | Migraine aura |
| **Symptom Duration** | Hours to <36 hours | Persistent (unless TIA) | Variable; resolves |
| **Brain Imaging** | Normal | Focal infarct/lesion | Normal |
| **Patient Age** | Any (common in pediatrics) | Often older / risk-driven | Often adolescent |

## Common Traps
- Assuming a stroke based on hemiplegia $\rightarrow$ always check for a preceding seizure.
- Mistaking conversion disorder for Todd paralysis $\rightarrow$ Conversion disorder lacks the preceding physiologic seizure event and is more common in adolescents with psychosocial triggers.
