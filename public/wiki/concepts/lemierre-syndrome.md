# Lemierre Syndrome

## Summary
Lemierre syndrome is a severe, life-threatening condition characterized by a primary oropharyngeal infection that leads to internal jugular vein thrombosis and subsequent septic pulmonary emboli. It typically affects healthy young adults.

## Pathophysiology
- **Causative Organism:** Most commonly caused by *Fusobacterium necrophorum* (a gram-negative anaerobic bacillus that is part of the normal oral flora).
- **Progression:** 
    1. Oropharyngeal infection (e.g., tonsillitis, dental work, or mastoiditis).
    2. Local invasion into the lateral pharyngeal space.
    3. Thrombophlebitis of the internal jugular vein.
    4. Dissemination of septic thromboemboli, most frequently to the lungs.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Initial Phase:** Prolonged sore throat (approx. 1 week), high fever, rigors, and dysphagia.
- **Neck Findings:** Pain, swelling, and tenderness along the sternocleidomastoid muscle.
- **Systemic Findings:** Toxic appearance, tachycardia, tachypnea.
- **Pulmonary Findings:** Respiratory distress, basilar crackles, and chest X-ray showing multiple peripheral lung nodules (often cavitary).

## Diagnostics
- **Imaging:** Duplex ultrasound of the neck to confirm internal jugular vein thrombosis.
- **Microbiology:** Blood or pus cultures to identify *F. necrophorum*.
- **Chest X-ray:** Peripheral cavitary nodules.

## Management
1. **Airway Management:** Support respiratory status if compromised.
2. **Antibiotics:** High-dose intravenous antibiotics.
3. **Surgical Intervention:** Incision and drainage or vein excision if the patient fails to respond to antibiotics.

## Differential Diagnosis
- **Epiglottitis:** Severe sore throat, drooling, and muffled voice, but lacks jugular vein thrombosis and septic pulmonary emboli.
- **Group A Strep Pharyngitis:** Presents with tonsillar exudates and is generally less toxic-appearing.
