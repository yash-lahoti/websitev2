# Alcoholic Hepatitis

## Summary
Inflammatory condition of the liver due to heavy alcohol consumption. Presents typically with jaundice, anorexia, and tender hepatomegaly.

## Laboratory Findings (Pathognomonic Pattern)
- **AST and ALT:** Modest elevations, typically **< 300 IU/L** and almost always **< 500 IU/L**.
- **AST/ALT Ratio:** **$\ge 2$** is very common (>90% of cases). This is attributed to hepatic deficiency of pyridoxal 5'-phosphate (a cofactor for ALT).
- **Other Markers:**
  - **GGT:** Elevated (Gamma-glutamyltransferase).
  - **Ferritin:** Elevated (Acute phase reactant).
  - **Bilirubin/INR:** May be elevated in severe cases.

## Differential Diagnosis of AST/ALT Elevations
- **Toxic/Ischemic/Viral Hepatitis:** Marked elevations typically **> 25x** the upper limit of normal (often > 1000 IU/L).
- **Other Liver Diseases:** Often show ALT > AST.

## Common Traps
- Expecting AST/ALT to be extremely high in alcoholic hepatitis $\rightarrow$ they are usually modest.
- Confusing the AST/ALT ratio with other liver diseases $\rightarrow$ Ratio $\ge 2$ is a strong indicator of alcoholic etiology.
