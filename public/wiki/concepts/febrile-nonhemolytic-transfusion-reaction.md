# Febrile Nonhemolytic Transfusion Reaction (FNHTR)

## Summary
FNHTR is the most common adverse transfusion reaction. It is a benign process caused by the release of cytokines from leukocytes within the stored blood product.

## Presentation / Exam Clues
- **Timing:** Typically develops within **1-6 hours** of transfusion initiation.
- **Symptoms:** Transient fever, chills, and malaise.
- **Diagnostics:** 
  - Negative Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT/Coombs).
  - Negative plasma-free hemoglobin levels.
  - No hemoglobinuria (unlike acute hemolytic reaction).

## Management & Prevention
- **Acute Management:** 
  - Immediate cessation of the transfusion (mandatory to rule out serious reactions).
  - Administration of antipyretics (e.g., acetaminophen).
- **Prevention:** Use of **leukoreduced blood products**. 
  - Leukoreduction filters out most leukocytes before storage, minimizing cytokine accumulation.
  - Also reduces risk of HLA alloimmunization and CMV transmission.

## Common Traps
- Confusing with Acute Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (AHTR) $\rightarrow$ AHTR has positive DAT, hemoglobinuria, and typically occurs faster (often within 1 hour).
- Thinking antihistamines/antipyretics prevent FNHTR $\rightarrow$ evidence shows they are not effective as prophylaxis.
