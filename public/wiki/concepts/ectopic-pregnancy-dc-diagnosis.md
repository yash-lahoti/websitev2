# Ectopic Pregnancy Diagnosis via D&C

## Summary
In cases of a pregnancy of unknown location (PUL) where $\beta$-hCG rise is abnormal (typically $<35\%$ over 48 hours), a diagnostic dilation and curettage (D&C) can be used to distinguish between a nonviable intrauterine pregnancy and an ectopic pregnancy.

## Diagnostic Logic (Post-D&C $\beta$-hCG)
1. **Decreasing or Negative $\beta$-hCG:** Confirms a **nonviable intrauterine pregnancy** (e.g., anembryonic gestation). The products of conception were removed.
2. **Persistent Rise in $\beta$-hCG:** Diagnostic for **ectopic pregnancy**. The uterus has been evacuated, but the extrauterine gestation continues to produce hCG.

## Management of Confirmed Ectopic Pregnancy
For hemodynamically stable patients with no contraindications (renal, hepatic, or hematologic disorders) and low $\beta$-hCG ($<5,000\text{ mIU/mL}$):
- **Medical Therapy:** **Methotrexate** (a folate antagonist).
- **Mechanism:** Inhibits DNA synthesis and cell growth in rapidly dividing cells (trophoblasts).
- **Follow-up:** Serial $\beta$-hCG monitoring until levels become undetectable.

## Differential: Nonviable Intrauterine Pregnancy
- Presents with a non-rising or slowly rising $\beta$-hCG.
- D&C removes the tissue, leading to a drop in $\beta$-hCG.
- Management is typically reassurance and observation.
