# Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)

## Summary
A group of disorders characterized by excessive collagen deposition and fibrosis in the perialveolar tissues (interstitium), leading to restrictive lung physiology.

## Presentation / Exam Clues
- **Symptoms:** Progressively worsening exertional dyspnea, persistent dry cough.
- **Physical Exam:** 
    - Fine, dry, late inspiratory crackles (Velcro crackles).
    - Digital clubbing (common in IPF).
- **Radiology:**
    - **CXR:** Diffuse reticular or nodular opacities.
    - **HRCT:** Honeycombing, traction bronchiectasis, and subpleural fibrosis.

## Workup / Management
- **PFTs:** Restrictive pattern.
    - $\downarrow$ FEV1, $\downarrow$ FVC.
    - FEV1/FVC ratio: Normal or $\uparrow$.
    - $\downarrow$ DLCO (due to thickened alveolar-capillary membrane).
    - $\downarrow$ Total Lung Capacity (TLC), $\downarrow$ Residual Volume (RV).
- **Gas Exchange:** 
    - Increased Alveolar-arterial (A-a) oxygen gradient $\rightarrow$ Hypoxemia (especially during exertion).
    - Hypercapnia ( $\uparrow$ PaCO2) is typically a late finding.
- **Diagnosis:** Clinical features + PFT + HRCT. Lung biopsy if diagnosis remains unclear.

## Common Traps
- **Obstructive vs Restrictive:** Do not confuse with obstructive diseases (where FEV1/FVC is decreased and RV/TLC are increased).
- **PaCO2:** CO2 diffuses faster than O2; therefore, hypoxemia occurs long before hypercapnia in ILD.
