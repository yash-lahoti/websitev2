# Sickle Cell Trait (SCT)

## Summary
A carrier state (HbAS) where individuals typically remain asymptomatic and have normal hemoglobin levels, but may experience "sickling" episodes under conditions of extreme physiologic stress.

## Presentation / Exam Clues
- **Typical State:** Asymptomatic; normal RBC indices and morphology.
- **Triggering Factors:** 
    - Low oxygen tension (e.g., high altitude).
    - Dehydration (e.g., severe heat).
    - Acidosis.
- **Renal Manifestations:**
    - **Renal Papillary Necrosis (RPN):** The renal medulla's low $pO_2$ and acidic pH promote sickling $\rightarrow$ ischemic injury $\rightarrow$ papillary sloughing.
    - **Clinical signs of RPN:** Painless gross hematuria, or acute flank pain.
    - **Ultrasound:** Increased echogenicity of the renal medulla.
    - **Other:** Potential for Renal Tubular Acidosis (impaired $H^+$ secretion).
- **Other Complications:**
    - Splenic infarction (especially at high altitude).
    - Venous thromboembolism.
    - Priapism.
    - Exertional rhabdomyolysis.

## Workup / Management
- **Lab Findings:** 
    - Hemoglobin electrophoresis: HbA > HbS.
    - Normal Hemoglobin and reticulocyte count (usually).
- **Management:** Avoid triggers (severe dehydration, extreme altitude without preparation).

## Common Traps
- **vs Sickle Cell Disease:** SCT patients do not have chronic hemolytic anemia or the usual severe complications of SCD (e.g., dactylitis, severe vaso-occlusive crises) unless under extreme stress.
- **RPN Cause:** While commonly associated with analgesic abuse (NSAIDs), SCT is a major cause of "spontaneous" RPN in specific populations.
