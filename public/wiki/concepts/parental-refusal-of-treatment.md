# Parental Refusal of Life-Saving Treatment

## Summary
While parents generally have the autonomy to make medical decisions for their minor children, this right is not absolute. Parents cannot legally refuse life-saving treatment for a child when that treatment is proven and likely to be effective.

## Legal and Ethical Framework
- **Best Interest Standard:** The primary goal is to act in the best interest of the child.
- **Parental Autonomy vs. Child's Right to Life:** The state's interest in protecting the life of the child overrides parental autonomy in cases of life-threatening conditions.

## Management Protocol
1. **Communication:** Continue engaging parents, explaining the benefits of treatment and the likely consequences of withholding it.
2. **Multidisciplinary Support:** Involve the hospital ethics committee, social services, and risk management to help parents overcome fears or misconceptions.
3. **Emergency vs. Non-Emergency:**
    - **Emergency:** If immediate delay is life-threatening, physicians are legally authorized to provide emergency treatment regardless of parental consent.
    - **Non-Emergency:** If the situation is stable but treatment is life-saving, the physician must seek a **court order** mandating the treatment.

## Summary Table: Management of Parental Refusal
| Situation | Action | Legal Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Emergency / Immediate Threat** | Treat immediately | Emergency exception to consent |
| **Non-Emergency / Life-Saving** | Petition for court order | State's *parens patriae* power to protect the child |
| **Non-Life-Threatening / Quality of Life** | Respect parental choice | Parental autonomy / Informed refusal |
