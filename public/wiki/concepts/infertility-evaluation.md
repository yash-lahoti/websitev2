# Infertility Evaluation

## Summary
Infertility is defined as the inability to conceive after **12 months** of regular, unprotected intercourse in women **< 35 years** (or 6 months in women $\ge 35$). Evaluation should begin promptly and involve both partners.

## Initial Evaluation Workflow
Evaluation of both partners is performed concurrently as male factor is responsible for up to 40% of cases.

### Male Factor Evaluation
- **Semen Analysis:** Primary initial test. Noninvasive and low-cost.
- **Measured parameters:**
    - **Concentration:** Sperm count and semen volume.
    - **Motility:** Percentage of sperm moving (essential for penetration).
    - **Morphology:** Shape and size (reflects spermatogenesis quality).

### Female Factor Evaluation
- **Ovulation Check:** 
    - Menstrual history (regularity).
    - **Midluteal phase progesterone** (Day 21).
- **Ovarian Reserve:**
    - **Antimüllerian hormone (AMH)**.
    - Day 3 FSH and estradiol.
    - Antral follicle count.
- **Anatomic Patency:**
    - **Hysterosalpingogram (HSG):** Gold standard for fallopian tube patency.
    - **Sonohysterogram:** Evaluates intrauterine anomalies.

## Common Traps
- Waiting too long to evaluate $\rightarrow$ Evaluation begins at 12 months (<35yo) or 6 months ($\ge 35$yo).
- Ignoring the male partner $\rightarrow$ Always start with semen analysis due to prevalence and ease of testing.
- Overestimating the yield of BBT $\rightarrow$ Basal body temperature is less reliable than midluteal progesterone for confirming ovulation.
