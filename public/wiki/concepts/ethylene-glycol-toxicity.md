# Ethylene Glycol Toxicity

## Summary
Ethylene glycol is a toxic alcohol found in antifreeze and brake fluid. Ingestion leads to metabolic acidosis and end-organ damage, particularly to the kidneys.

## Pathophysiology
- **Metabolism:** Ethylene glycol is metabolized by **alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH)** $\rightarrow$ glycolic acid $\rightarrow$ oxalic acid.
- **Toxicity:** The organic acids cause a high-anion gap metabolic acidosis (HAGMA). Oxalic acid precipitates with calcium to form **calcium oxalate crystals**, leading to hypocalcemia and acute kidney injury (AKI).

## Clinical Presentation
1. **Early Phase:** CNS sedation and inebriation (mimics ethanol intoxication), osmolar gap.
2. **Late Phase:** 
   - **Metabolic:** Anion gap metabolic acidosis (AGMA), compensatory tachypnea.
   - **Renal:** Acute kidney injury (AKI), flank pain, bloody urine.
   - **Urinalysis:** Numerous calcium oxalate crystals.
   - **Lab:** Hypocalcemia.

## Management
1. **ADH Inhibition:** Prevent the formation of toxic metabolites.
   - **First-line:** Fomepizole.
   - **Alternative:** Ethanol (competes for ADH).
2. **Acidemia Correction:** IV sodium bicarbonate (especially if pH < 7.3) to reduce tissue penetration of toxins.
3. **Toxin Removal:** Hemodialysis for severe acidosis or end-organ damage (AKI).
