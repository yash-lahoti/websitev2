# Smoking Cessation Supportive Therapy

## Summary
Effective smoking cessation requires a combination of behavioral therapy, pharmacotherapy, and supportive physician-patient interaction. Supportive therapy aims to reduce barriers and provide encouragement.

## Key Components of Supportive Therapy
- **Education:** Discuss the process of quitting, selecting a quit date, and choosing the right pharmacotherapy.
- **Normalization of Failure:** Remind patients that initial failure is common and that multiple attempts are often necessary before achieving long-term success.
- **Reassurance:** Provide evidence-based hope; for example, reassure patients that over half of smokers eventually achieve long-term abstinence.
- **Addressing Barriers:** Actively identify and address specific barriers, such as nicotine withdrawal symptoms (e.g., jitteriness).

## Physician Communication
- **Avoid Dismissiveness:** Avoid phrases like "Don't worry" or "Forget about it," which can be perceived as dismissive of the patient's genuine struggles.
- **Avoid Judgment:** Avoid telling the patient they are "not trying hard enough," as this is unempathetic and reinforces the fear of being seen as a failure.
- **Proactive Engagement:** The physician should encourage cessation at every visit, rather than waiting for the patient to initiate the conversation.
