# Cocaine-Induced Coronary Ischemia

## Summary
Cocaine is a sympathomimetic agent that increases myocardial oxygen demand and reduces supply via coronary vasoconstriction, leading to ischemia or STEMI.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Sympathetic Hyperactivity:** Tachycardia, hypertension, dilated pupils, psychomotor agitation, diaphoresis.
- **Cardiac:** Chest pain (substernal pressure), ST-segment elevations on ECG.
- **Other Complications:** Aortic dissection, intracranial hemorrhage (ICH), pulmonary infarction.

## Management
- **First-line:** Intravenous **benzodiazepines** to reduce agitation, tachycardia, and hypertension.
- **Ischemic Chest Pain:** 
	- Aspirin (antiplatelet).
	- Nitroglycerin and calcium channel blockers (to counteract vasoconstriction).
	- Cardiac catheterization/PCI for STEMI.
- **Contraindicated/Caution:** 
	- **Beta-blockers:** Avoided due to theoretical risk of "unopposed alpha-stimulation" leading to worsened coronary vasoconstriction.
	- **Fibrinolysis:** Caution due to increased risk of ICH.
