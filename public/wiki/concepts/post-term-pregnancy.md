# Post-term Pregnancy

## Definitions
- **Late-term pregnancy:** $\ge 41$ weeks gestation.
- **Post-term pregnancy:** $\ge 42$ weeks gestation.

## Pathophysiology
Post-term pregnancies are associated with age-related placental changes (e.g., infarctions, calcifications), which increase placental vascular resistance. This leads to **uteroplacental insufficiency**, resulting in chronic fetal hypoxemia.

To protect the fetal brain, blood is preferentially distributed to the CNS and away from peripheral tissues, including the kidneys.

## Complications
### Fetal/Neonatal
- **Oligohydramnios:** Reduced renal perfusion $\rightarrow$ decreased fetal urine production $\rightarrow$ low amniotic fluid. This is a critical marker of placental insufficiency.
- **Fetal Macrosomia:** Increased risk of weight $\ge 4.5\text{ kg}$.
- **Dysmaturity Syndrome:** Result of chronic uteroplacental insufficiency.
- **Intrauterine Fetal Demise (IUFD):** Final consequence of severe placental failure.

### Maternal
- **Obstetric Lacerations:** Increased risk due to fetal macrosomia.
- **Cesarean Delivery:** Higher rate due to failed induction or fetal distress.
- **Postpartum Hemorrhage:** Increased risk associated with uterine overdistension.

## Management
- **Monitoring:** Frequent fetal surveillance (e.g., Nonstress Tests, amniotic fluid index via ultrasound) to detect uteroplacental insufficiency.
- **Delivery:** Delivery is generally indicated prior to 43 weeks gestation to prevent IUFD.
- **Indications for Immediate Delivery:**
    - Oligohydramnios (SDP $< 2\text{ cm}$ or AFI $< 5\text{ cm}$).
    - Abnormal fetal monitoring (e.g., late decelerations).
