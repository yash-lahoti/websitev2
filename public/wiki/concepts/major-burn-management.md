# Major Burn Management

## Summary
Management of major burns (>20% TBSA) focuses on aggressive fluid resuscitation to prevent hypovolemic shock, stabilization of ABCs, and meticulous monitoring of organ perfusion.

## Initial Management
1. **Stabilization:** ABCs (Airway, Breathing, Circulation).
2. **Fluid Resuscitation:** 
    - Initiated based on formulas (e.g., Parkland formula).
    - Titrated to maintain adequate urine output (Goal: $\ge 0.5$ mL/kg/hr).
3. **Urinary Catheterization:** 
    - **Requirement:** Mandatory for any patient requiring aggressive fluid resuscitation.
    - **Timing:** Should be placed as soon as possible, especially in patients with **genital burns**.
    - **Why:** To ensure accurate measurement of urine output and prevent urine from contaminating burned areas/dressings. Delay can lead to edema that makes catheterization difficult.

## Wound Care
- **Immediate:** Copious irrigation and gentle gauze debridement.
- **Topical Therapy:** Application of topical antimicrobial agents and nonstick dressings.
- **Surgical Debridement:** Sharp surgical debridement and skin grafting are typically deferred for $\sim 72$ hours until burns have fully demarcated.

## Contraindications & Traps
- **No Ice:** Avoid ice application as it can cause further tissue injury (frostbite) and systemic hypothermia. Use cool, saline-soaked gauze instead.
- **Surgical Timing:** Do not perform sharp debridement immediately; wait for demarcation.
