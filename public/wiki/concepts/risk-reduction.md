# Risk Reduction Measures

## Absolute Risk Reduction (ARR)
The absolute difference in the event rate between the control group and the treatment group.
- **Formula:** $\text{ARR} = \text{Control Rate} - \text{Treatment Rate}$
- **Meaning:** Indicates the actual percentage decrease in risk.

## Relative Risk Reduction (RRR)
The proportion of the control group's risk that is removed by the treatment.
- **Formula 1:** $\text{RRR} = \frac{\text{ARR}}{\text{Control Rate}} = \frac{\text{Control Rate} - \text{Treatment Rate}}{\text{Control Rate}}$
- **Formula 2:** $\text{RRR} = 1 - \text{Relative Risk (RR)}$
- **Meaning:** Describes how much the risk is reduced *relative* to the original risk.

## Relative Risk (RR)
The ratio of the probability of an event occurring in the treatment group compared to the control group.
- **Formula:** $\text{RR} = \frac{\text{Treatment Rate}}{\text{Control Rate}}$

## Number Needed to Treat (NNT)
The number of patients that need to be treated to prevent one additional bad outcome.
- **Formula:** $\text{NNT} = \frac{1}{\text{ARR}}$
