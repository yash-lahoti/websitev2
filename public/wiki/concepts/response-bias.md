# Response Bias

## Summary
Response bias occurs when survey participants provide inaccurate or untruthful answers, often to make themselves appear more desirable or to avoid stigma associated with sensitive topics (e.g., substance use, sexual behavior).

## Key Characteristics
- **Context:** Most common in cross-sectional studies (surveys, polls, interviews).
- **Mechanism:** Participants purposely give "socially desirable" responses.
- **Impact:** Results in inaccurate data, often underestimating the prevalence of risk factors or stigmatized diseases.

## Contrast with Related Biases
- **Nonresponse Bias:** Occurs when the people who *do not* respond to a survey differ systematically from those who *do* respond. (Response bias is about the *quality* of the answer; Nonresponse is about *who* answered).
- **Recall Bias:** Occurs when participants inaccurately remember past events (common in retrospective case-control studies).

## Common Traps
- Confusing Response Bias with Nonresponse Bias $\rightarrow$ remember: Response = "lying/wrong answer"; Nonresponse = "missing person".
- Assuming a high response rate eliminates response bias $\rightarrow$ Even if 100% of people respond, they can still lie.
