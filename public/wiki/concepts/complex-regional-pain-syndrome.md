# Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS)

## Summary
Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS) is a chronic pain condition that typically follows a localized trauma (e.g., fracture, sprain) or surgery. It is characterized by pain that is disproportionate to the inciting event and affects a regional (non-dermatomal) distribution.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Pain Quality:** Severe, constant, burning or stinging.
- **Allodynia:** Severe pain elicited by light touch.
- **Autonomic Changes:**
    - **Vasomotor:** Erythema, altered skin temperature.
    - **Sudomotor:** Abnormal sweating.
- **Physical Findings:**
    - Nonpitting edema.
    - Trophic changes (affecting skin, nails, and hair).
    - Decreased range of motion, motor weakness, or contractures (in advanced cases).
- **Imaging:** X-ray may show **patchy demineralization** (osteopenia) in the affected limb.

## Management
- **Therapy:** Strong emphasis on physical and occupational therapy, exercise, and patient education.
- **Psychological Support:** Address comorbid depression and anxiety.
- **Pharmacotherapy:**
    - NSAIDs.
    - Antineuropathic agents: Pregabalin, Tricyclic antidepressants (e.g., amitriptyline).
    - Others: Bisphosphonates, topical lidocaine, or capsaicin.

## Common Traps
- Confusing with Cellulitis $\rightarrow$ CRPS lacks fever, leukocytosis, and elevated inflammatory markers (ESR/CRP).
- Confusing with DVT $\rightarrow$ CRPS has allodynia and patchy osteopenia, which are not seen in DVT.
- Confusing with Peripheral Neuropathy $\rightarrow$ Neuropathy usually lacks edema, erythema, and osteopenia, and is typically bilateral (stocking-glove).
