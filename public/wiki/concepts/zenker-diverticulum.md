# Zenker Diverticulum

## Summary
Zenker diverticulum is a pulsion pseudodiverticulum occurring at the posterior wall of the cervical esophagus, specifically at the pharyngoesophageal junction (Killian's triangle). It is caused by impaired relaxation or abnormal spasm of the cricopharyngeus muscle.

## Pathophysiology
- **Mechanism:** Pulsion (increased intraluminal pressure) leads to mucosal herniation through a point of weakness.
- **Location:** Posterior to the esophagus, just above the upper esophageal sphincter.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Dysphagia:** Difficulty swallowing, often described as food getting "stuck" in the throat.
- **Regurgitation:** Regurgitation of undigested food or medications, often occurring several hours after eating or when lying supine.
- **Halitosis:** Bad breath due to trapped food in the pouch.
- **Aspiration:** Risk of aspiration pneumonia from regurgitated contents.

## Diagnostics
- **Barium Esophagogram:** The gold standard. Shows a posterior outpouching of the cervical esophagus.

## Management
- **Surgical Treatment:** The definitive treatment for symptomatic patients is **cricopharyngeal myotomy** (surgical division of the cricopharyngeus muscle).
- **Optional:** Diverticulectomy (removal of the pouch) may be performed in conjunction with the myotomy.

## Differential Diagnosis
| Condition | Key Differentiator |
|---|---|
| **Esophageal Stricture** | Smoothly tapering narrowing on barium swallow; dysphagia usually limited to solids. |
| **Vascular Ring** | External compression of the esophagus by anomalous vessels; not a pouch. |
| **Oropharyngeal Dysphagia** | Neurologic origin; treated with swallowing rehabilitation. |
