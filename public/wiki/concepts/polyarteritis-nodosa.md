# Polyarteritis nodosa (PAN)

## Summary
A systemic necrotizing vasculitis affecting medium-sized muscular arteries. It is characterized by segmental, transmural inflammation that damages the elastic laminae, leading to luminal narrowing and the formation of microaneurysms.

## Presentation / Exam Clues
- **Constitutional:** Fever, weight loss, malaise, joint pain (myalgias/arthritis).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Intermittent abdominal pain after eating (mesenteric ischemia), weight loss, possible bowel perforation.
- **Renal:** Hypertension, renal insufficiency, renal infarcts.
- **Skin:** Livedo reticularis, subcutaneous nodules, ulcers, purpura.
- **Neurologic:** Mononeuritis multiplex, seizures, headache.

## Workup / Management
- **Angiography (Mesenteric/Renal):** Gold standard for identifying characteristic "beading" (microaneurysms, irregular narrowing, and distal occlusions).
- **Biopsy:** Gold standard for definitive diagnosis; shows nongranulomatous transmural inflammation and fibrinoid necrosis.
- **Laboratory:** Typically ANA and ANCA negative.
- **Associations:** Strong correlation with Hepatitis B infection.

## Common Traps
- **vs. Atherosclerosis:** PAN presents with systemic inflammatory symptoms (fever, joint pain) and microaneurysms, which are atypical for atherosclerosis.
- **vs. Bacterial Endocarditis:** PAN has negative blood cultures and characteristic angiographic beading.
- **vs. Fibromuscular Dysplasia:** FMD is more common in women and typically lacks systemic inflammatory symptoms.
