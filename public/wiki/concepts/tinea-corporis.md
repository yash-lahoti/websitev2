# Tinea Corporis

## Summary
A superficial dermatophyte infection (typically *Trichophyton rubrum*) characterized by scaly, annular (ring-like) plaques.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Appearance:** Scaly, annular plaques with central clearing.
- **Symptoms:** Often pruritic (itchy).
- **Spread:** Can spread via autoinoculation to other sites:
	- **Tinea pedis** (toes)
	- **Tinea cruris** (groin)
	- **Tinea unguium / Onychomycosis** (nails)

## Workup
- **KOH Prep:** Skin scrapings showing segmented hyphae.
- **Red Flag:** Extensive or widespread involvement should raise suspicion for **compromised immunity**, including:
	- Diabetes mellitus.
	- Systemic glucocorticoid therapy.
	- **HIV infection.**

## Management
- **Localized Disease:** Topical antifungals.
- **Extensive Disease:** Systemic antifungals (e.g., oral fluconazole).
- **Additional Workup:** Screen for underlying causes of immunosuppression (e.g., HIV screening) in cases of widespread infection.
