# Narcolepsy

## Summary
A chronic sleep disorder characterized by the brain's inability to regulate sleep-wake cycles, leading to excessive daytime sleepiness and sudden transitions into REM sleep.

## Presentation / Exam Clues
- **Core Symptoms (The Tetrad):**
    - **Excessive Daytime Sleepiness (EDS):** Irresistible need to nap; falling asleep during active tasks (e.g., talking, driving).
    - **Cataplexy:** Sudden, emotionally triggered loss of muscle tone (e.g., laughing, surprise).
    - **Hypnagogic / Hypnopompic Hallucinations:** Vivid, often frightening dreams occurring upon falling asleep (hypnagogic) or waking up (hypnopompic).
    - **Sleep Paralysis:** Temporary inability to move or speak upon awakening or falling asleep.
- **Physical Exam:** Often unremarkable; obesity may be present.

## Workup / Management
- **Diagnosis:** Clinical history; confirmed by Polysomnography (PSG) and Multiple Sleep Latency Test (MSLT) showing short sleep latency and rapid REM onset.
- **Non-Pharmacological:** 
    - Sleep hygiene.
    - Scheduled short naps.
    - Avoidance of sedating drugs/alcohol.
- **Pharmacological:**
    - **First-line for wakefulness:** **Modafinil** (non-amphetamine, lower abuse potential, well-tolerated).
    - **Second-line for wakefulness:** Traditional stimulants (Amphetamines, Methylphenidate).
    - **Cataplexy management:** REM-suppressing drugs (e.g., antidepressants, sodium oxybate).

## Common Traps
- **vs Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA):** OSA also presents with daytime fatigue, but lacks the pathognomonic REM-related symptoms (cataplexy, hallucinations, sleep paralysis). Treatment for OSA is CPAP.
- **Treatment Missteps:** Avoid sedating agents (benzodiazepines, antipsychotics), as they exacerbate daytime somnolence.
