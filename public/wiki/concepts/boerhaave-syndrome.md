# Boerhaave Syndrome (Effort Rupture of the Esophagus)

## Summary
Boerhaave syndrome is a spontaneous transmural perforation of the esophagus, typically occurring after forceful vomiting or retching. This leads to the leakage of gastric contents into the mediastinum and pleural space, causing severe inflammation and sepsis.

## Pathophysiology
- **Mechanism:** A sudden increase in intrathoracic and intra-abdominal pressure (during vomiting) while the upper esophageal sphincter is closed, leading to an "effort rupture."
- **Location:** Most common in the **left posterolateral aspect of the distal esophagus** due to inherent anatomic weakness.
- **Complication:** Leakage into the pleural space frequently results in a **left-sided pleural effusion**.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Classic Triad:**
    1. **Vomiting/Retching:** The triggering event.
    2. **Severe Chest/Back Pain:** Often sudden onset, constant, and severe.
    3. **Systemic Response:** Fever, tachycardia, and signs of sepsis.
- **Physical Exam Signs:**
    - **Hamman sign:** A "crunching" sound heard on auscultation over the heart, synchronous with the heartbeat (due to pneumomediastinum).
    - **Subcutaneous emphysema:** Crepitus felt in the neck or chest wall.
    - **Lung findings:** Dullness to percussion and decreased breath sounds (if pleural effusion is present).

## Diagnostics
- **Chest X-ray:**
    - Widened mediastinum.
    - Pneumomediastinum or pneumothorax.
    - Unilateral pleural effusion (usually left).
- **Definitive Diagnosis:** **Esophagography with water-soluble contrast** (e.g., Gastrografin).
    - Contrast extravasation confirms the leak.
    - *Note:* Barium is generally avoided initially as it is more inflammatory to the mediastinum.
- **Alternative:** CT scan of the chest with water-soluble contrast.

## Management
- **Urgent Action:** Emergent surgical consultation.
- **Medical Stabilization:**
    - NPO (Nothing by mouth).
    - Broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics.
    - Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs).
- **Surgical Intervention:** Operative debridement of necrotic tissue and primary repair of the perforation.

## Differential Diagnosis
| Condition | Distinguishing Feature |
|---|---|
| **Mallory-Weiss Tear** | Mucosal tear (not transmural); presents with hematemesis without systemic sepsis or pleural effusion. |
| **Aortic Dissection** | Tearing chest/back pain; associated with BP differential or new murmur; fever is uncommon. |
| **Aspiration Pneumonia** | Fever and lung infiltrates; usually lacks sudden severe chest pain or pneumomediastinum. |
