# Alcohol Withdrawal Syndrome

## Summary
Alcohol withdrawal occurs after abrupt cessation of alcohol intake in a dependent individual. It manifests as a spectrum of symptoms that evolve over time.

## Timeline of Withdrawal

| Phase | Onset | Key Features | Mental State | Vitals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mild Withdrawal** | 6–24 hr | Anxiety, insomnia, tremors, diaphoresis, palpitations, GI upset | Intact orientation | Relatively stable |
| **Alcoholic Hallucinosis** | 12–48 hr | Visual, auditory, or tactile hallucinations | **Intact orientation** | **Stable** |
| **Withdrawal Seizures** | 12–48 hr | Generalized tonic-clonic seizures | N/A | Variable |
| **Delirium Tremens (DTs)** | 48–96 hr | Confusion, agitation, fever, hallucinations | **Disoriented / Delirious** | **Hyperactive (Tachy/HTN)** |

## Key Distinction: Hallucinosis vs. DTs
- **Alcoholic Hallucinosis**: Patient is **alert and oriented**. Vital signs are stable. Often resolves within 48 hours.
- **Delirium Tremens**: Patient is **disoriented/confused**. Marked sympathetic hyperactivity (tachycardia, hypertension, fever).

## Management
- **Benzodiazepines**: First-line for all phases to prevent progression to seizures and DTs.
- **Supportive care**: Thiamine (to prevent Wernicke-Korsakoff), fluids, and nutrition.

## Common Traps
- Confusing Hallucinosis with DTs $\rightarrow$ check orientation and vitals.
- Assuming hallucinations always mean DTs $\rightarrow$ timing and mental status are key.
