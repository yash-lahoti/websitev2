# Nodular Malignant Melanoma

## Summary
Nodular melanoma is a subtype of malignant melanoma that grows primarily **vertically** (downward) rather than horizontally. This makes it more aggressive and potentially more lethal than superficial spreading melanoma.

## Clinical Features
- **Appearance:** Typically a darkly pigmented, symmetric, firm, and elevated nodule.
- **ABCDE Criteria:** Often **less sensitive** for nodular melanoma because it lacks the asymmetry, irregular borders, and color variation characteristic of superficial spreading types.
- **Key Indicators for Suspicion:**
    - **"Ugly Duckling Sign":** A lesion that looks significantly different from all other pigmented lesions on the patient's body.
    - **Elevation:** Nodular or pedunculated growth.
    - **Growth:** Continuous growth over a one-month period.
    - **Palpation:** Firmness to touch.

## Diagnosis & Management
- **Gold Standard:** Full-thickness excisional biopsy.
- **Key Goal:** Determining the **Breslow depth** (thickness of the tumor), which is the most important prognostic factor.

## Common Traps
- Relying solely on ABCDE criteria $\rightarrow$ these are designed for superficial spreading melanoma; nodular melanoma can be symmetric and uniform in color.
- Confusing with Seborrheic Keratosis $\rightarrow$ SK typically has a "stuck-on" appearance and velvety texture.
