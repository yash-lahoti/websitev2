# Bipolar II Disorder

## Summary
Bipolar II disorder is characterized by at least one **hypomanic episode** and at least one **major depressive episode**. Unlike Bipolar I, there are no full-blown manic episodes.

## Hypomania vs. Mania
- **Hypomania (Bipolar II):**
    - Duration: $\ge 4$ consecutive days.
    - Symptoms: Elevated mood, increased energy, decreased need for sleep, increased goal-directed activity, inflated self-esteem.
    - Impairment: Symptoms are observable by others but **not** severe enough to cause marked functional impairment or necessitate hospitalization.
    - Psychosis: **No** psychotic features.
- **Mania (Bipolar I):**
    - Duration: $\ge 1$ week (or any duration if hospitalized).
    - Impairment: Marked functional impairment in social or occupational functioning.
    - Psychosis: May include psychotic features.

## Differential Diagnosis: Bipolar II vs. Borderline Personality Disorder (BPD)
- **Mood Stability:** 
    - **Bipolar II:** Mood states are episodic and typically last weeks to months.
    - **BPD:** Mood is highly labile and fluctuates rapidly (hours to days) in response to interpersonal stressors.
- **Core Patterns:** BPD is characterized by a lifelong pattern of identity disturbance, fear of abandonment, splitting, and impulsive self-mutilation, which are not central to Bipolar II.

## Common Traps
- Misdiagnosing Bipolar II as Major Depressive Disorder $\rightarrow$ check for a history of hypomania (often under-reported by patients).
- Confusing Bipolar II with BPD $\rightarrow$ evaluate the duration and trigger of mood shifts.
