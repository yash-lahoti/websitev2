# Cephalopelvic Disproportion (CPD)

## Summary
Cephalopelvic disproportion occurs when the fetal head is too large, or the maternal pelvis is too small/irregular, to allow the fetus to pass through the birth canal.

## Clinical Significance
- CPD is a primary cause of **active phase protraction** and **arrest of labor**.
- In the active phase (6-10 cm), CPD results in a dilation rate of **< 1 cm every 2 hours** despite adequate uterine contractions.

## Risk Factors
- **Fetal:**
    - Macrosomia (common in gestational diabetes).
    - Late-term pregnancy ($\ge$ 41 weeks).
    - Malposition (e.g., occiput posterior).
    - Fetal anomalies (e.g., hydrocephalus).
- **Maternal:**
    - Small pelvic diameter (e.g., android or platypelloid pelvis).
    - Obesity and excessive weight gain.
    - Advanced maternal age.

## Diagnostic Approach
- Suggested by **protracted labor** in the presence of **adequate uterine contractions** ($\ge$ 200 Montevideo units).
- Confirmed if progress does not improve with oxytocin or fetal position correction.

## Management
- If CPD is confirmed and labor does not progress, **Cesarean delivery** is indicated.
